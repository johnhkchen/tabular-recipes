# Occasions

**An occasion is a moment somebody sells for.** Not a theme, not a cuisine, not a mood, not a
season with recipes attached — a recurring moment that a caterer prints a menu for, a chain prints
an order form for, a bakery bakes only during, or a restaurant opens a Sunday for.

This file is [counters.md](counters.md) for moments, and it carries that file's opening rule
unchanged: **archetypes, not taxonomies.** Counters were settled from roughly seventy real menus
read end to end rather than from the recipes already written. Occasions are settled the same way and
from the same kind of evidence — what somebody sells — rather than from the recipes this collection
happens to hold.

Sibling arguments: [counters.md](counters.md) settled what a counter is,
[scaling.md](scaling.md) settled what cooking more of a thing costs, [cooks.md](cooks.md) settled
who is standing in the kitchen, and [voice.md](voice.md) settled how any of it may be said out loud.
The counts here are from 7 August 2026, over 685 recipe files.

**What an occasion is not**, said first because every wrong answer in this area is one of these
four:

- **Not a theme.** *A rustic Tuscan evening* is an idea about a dinner. Nobody sells one, which is
  why the cookbook of them goes unopened — and why the recipes in it were chosen for agreeing with
  the idea rather than for what anybody eats.
- **Not a cuisine.** Italian is not a moment. It is a shelf, and the shelf already exists.
- **Not a mood.** *Cosy*, *festive* and *impressive* describe the writer, not the evening.
- **Not a season with recipes attached.** Autumn is not an occasion. Thanksgiving is. The difference
  is a date, a deadline, and somebody taking a deposit against it.

## 1. Somebody sells for it

**The rule, and it is the whole filter: an occasion is real if somebody sells for it.** Nobody sells
for a rustic Tuscan evening. Somebody sells, at scale and against a deadline, for the fourth
Thursday in November.

The rule is worth stating this baldly because it is the one the occasion cookbook never applied. A
book organised by theme selects for coherence with an idea; roughly three-quarters of it is dead
weight by construction, because the idea is wrong three seasons out of four. Selling evidence
selects for what people actually pay for, which is the same move `counters.md` made and the reason
that file's twenty-one archetypes are archetypes rather than a category list.

### The four kinds of evidence

Four, and they are kept as four because **each fails differently**, so agreement between them means
something.

| Kind | What was found | What it proves | What it cannot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A pre-order sheet with a deadline** | A Huntsville supermarket's ham & turkey order form, orders due 21 December; a diner's Thanksgiving feast for 1, 4 or 8 with a 25 November cut-off; a chain pricing holiday entrées **by the pound** | The strongest of the four. A form commits inventory: a chain that prints one has forecast demand it intends to buy against | Say anything about a moment too small or too private to pre-order for |
| **A caterer's seasonal menu** | A holiday combo from $45 a head with a ten-person minimum; family feasts sold in sizes of four and ten; three tiered holiday packages | A caterer who lists a dish nobody orders stops listing it, so a menu that survives a second year is a measurement | Distinguish an aspirational package from one anybody bought. A first-year menu proves nothing |
| **A seasonal bakery board** | Hot cross buns listed as *seasonal, January through April*; pan de muerto in Mexican bakeries from mid-September; mooncake boxes appearing in Asian supermarkets in the weeks before the festival | The purest case there is: the dish exists **only** for the moment, so the moment is the entire reason it is baked | Say how big the moment is. One bakery's board is one bakery |
| **A one-night prix fixe, and what dining volume does** | Two decades of National Restaurant Association reading put **Mother's Day first** among dining-out days, then Valentine's, Father's Day, New Year's Eve and Easter; 80 million US adults were forecast to eat out on Mother's Day 2026 | The only kind that measures a whole population rather than one shop | Say what anybody cooks. A day people eat out is a day people did not cook, which for this collection is a warning as much as a signal |

### What is not evidence, and this is where the rule does its rejecting

**Advice is not selling.** A moving company's blog post about what to eat on moving day, a listicle
of thirty make-ahead meals for houseguests, a magazine's ideas for a cosy evening — none of these is
anybody committing money to the moment. They are *content about* a moment, which is exactly what the
occasion cookbook is. Admitting them readmits the failure this file exists to avoid.

**A tradition is not selling either.** Moving day has a tradition — pizza, or Chinese takeout, in
some families every time — and it produces no product. Nobody prints a moving-day menu. That gap
between a real habit and no product is the clearest demonstration that the two are different things.

**And what people look up is not what people buy.** Search volume measures curiosity.
[cooks.md](cooks.md)'s second cook looks up a great deal more than they cook, and their whole
complaint is that the looking is the cost.

### Applied

| Candidate | What was found | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A holiday meal — Thanksgiving, Christmas | All four kinds, abundantly | **Real** |
| A dumpling party | Make-your-own dumpling sets at Costco and Target; ticketed dumpling-making parties around $55 for two hours; virtual classes taught by name chefs | **Real** — and note what is sold: the *making*, not the dumplings |
| A new baby | Postpartum meal delivery priced by the week — one-to-four-week packages, roughly $195–665 a week; new-parent meal gift packages from $99 | **Real**, thinner. Regional, and priced as a gift rather than as a household's own shop |
| Somebody died | *Repast* is a printed menu category at delis and caterers — sandwich platters, deli trays, hot entrées, dessert trays, priced as packages | **Real** |
| A snow day | Restaurant snow-day specials across four cities; pizzerias selling take-home *Snow Day* kits | **Real by this rule.** See §2 — real is not the same as ours |
| **Moving day** | Movers' blogs and advice posts, and the pizza tradition. **No menu, no form, no deadline, no priced package** | **Rejected.** Advice about what to eat is not somebody selling for it |
| **In-laws for a week** | Hosting blogs and make-ahead listicles. The nearest priced product is an Airbnb host-services *welcome meal*, sold to a lodging business for an arrival day | **Rejected** |
| A rustic Tuscan evening | Not sought | **Rejected** — the definitional case |

**Two of those three rejections are inside this site's own lists, and they stay rejected.** *Moving*
is on [S-013](../active/stories/S-013-cooking-for-a-moment.md)'s own list of moments in life.
*In-laws for a week* is one of the three people in [cooks.md](cooks.md). A rule that rejects nothing
is not a rule, and a rule that rejects only other people's ideas is worse than none.

**The houseguests case is worth one more sentence, because it is the subtle one.** The cook is real
— they came from a person, and `cooks.md` records them. The *week* is not an occasion. What sells,
and sells hard, is the **big meal inside that week**, which is a time-of-year occasion the household
happens to be hosting. So the rejection costs nothing: the moment that reader actually needs help
with is admitted on the other axis.

### The second gate: real is not the same as ours

The snow day was expected to be the rejection and it is not. Restaurants print snow-day specials;
somebody sells take-home kits called that. It passes.

So the file states two gates in order, and they are different questions:

1. **Is it real?** Somebody sells for it. This is a question about the world.
2. **Is it ours?** Does this site answer it better than something already on the board does. This is
   a question about the board.

An occasion can pass the first and fail the second, and one does. Collapsing the two would force a
choice between admitting the snow day and duplicating a shipped feature, or bending the evidence to
keep it out. Neither is honest.

## 2. The three axes

### Time of year

**In.** The holidays and the seasonal board. It recurs on a calendar, it recurs for the same
household every year, and the evidence is not merely present but of all four kinds at once: the form
with the deadline, the caterer's package, the board that exists for six weeks, and the busiest
restaurant day of the year.

It is also the axis whose failure is most predictable, which is the point S-013 opens on: *why is it
the third week of November and I am underwater* has the same answer every year for the same
household, and nothing on this site says it.

### Moment in life

**In, and the evidence is graded rather than assumed.** A new baby and a death both have real
priced products behind them — meal delivery sold by the week, a repast menu printed at a deli. That
is two of the four kinds. There is **no pre-order sheet** and **no dining-volume figure** for either,
because neither is scheduled far enough ahead to pre-order for and neither is a day anybody books a
table for.

So it is admitted on thinner evidence, and this file says thinner rather than pretending otherwise.
What follows practically: a moment-in-life occasion should not be given the confidence a holiday
gets, and one whose only support is a listicle is not one of these.

**Illness and a meal train** sit here on the same footing and were not separately searched. They are
recorded as plausible and unverified rather than assumed real.

### Type of day

**Out — and not because it failed the selling test.** It passed. The snow day has restaurant
specials and take-home kits. This is the axis the ticket that produced this file warned was
expensive to get wrong in either direction, so both directions are stated.

**Why out.** [S-010](../active/stories/S-010-after-a-long-day.md) already answers it, and answers it
better than a shelf could. Its three dials are:

| Dial | What it reads | Coverage |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Time you're standing there | `handsOnMinutes`, with `assumedHandsOnMinutes` saying how much nobody claimed | 269 of 685 can speak |
| On the table by | the critical path, as a cap rather than a target | nearly all |
| Things to wash | the count derived from `>> washing-up:` | 177 of 685 |

and one more the same story derives, `longestHandsOnMinutes` — *thirty unbroken minutes at the hob
against three ten-minute jobs* — which is the exact distinction *a long day* is made of.

A *long day* shelf would be those four numbers with a fixed threshold, chosen by us, printed as a
list. That is strictly less than the dials: the reader loses the ability to say *forty minutes is
fine, thirty at a pan is not*, which is the whole sentence S-010 is built on.

**What the decision costs, both ways.**

- **Admitted as an occasion**, it duplicates a shipped feature and hands one question two places to
  be asked. `cooks.md`'s second cook — whose cost **is** the deciding — is made worse by exactly
  that, because a second surface is another decision.
- **Excluded**, it risks stranding the person most often in the kitchen. **This is the cost being
  accepted**, and it is accepted because they are not stranded: they are the reader S-010 was built
  for, and `cooks.md`'s worked verdict already records that the three dials **pass** for them. The
  cook is served; the shelf would not add to it.

**What is deliberately not proposed here.** Pre-set dial positions — a *long day* button that sets
the standing-time dial — are a real idea and they belong to T-011-06, which S-011 already owns. A
knowledge file that starts recommending features stops being the thing later work can be tested
against.

## 3. What makes a hall of famer, and why it is per occasion

**This is the section the file earns its keep with.** A hall of famer is not a good recipe. It is a
recipe that survives *that moment's* constraints, and the constraints are measurable.

The tempting mistake is a difficulty score with a season attached — rank everything by easy-to-hard
and change the header per holiday. It is wrong, and the dumpling party is the decisive
counter-example: a filter tuned for a big family meal ranks the dumpling party's best dish dead
last, because **the labour is the party**.

### 3.1 The fields a profile is allowed to use

Every one exists today. The coverage column is what stops a profile pretending.

| Field | Where it comes from | Declared on 685 |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| `handsOnMinutes` | `src/lib/schedule.ts` — summed over branches | all |
| `assumedHandsOnMinutes` | same — how much of the above nobody claimed | all |
| `handsOnEvidence()` | same — `stated` · `inferred` · `unknown` | **269 not `unknown`** |
| `longestHandsOnMinutes` | same — the longest unbroken run, measured on one cook | all |
| `totalMinutes` | same — the critical path, not a sum | all |
| `untimedCount` | same — operations that never said how long | all |
| `slack` | `src/lib/slack.ts` — a level **and** a reason | **416** |
| `washingUp.count` | `src/lib/washing-up.ts` — derived from an authored list | **177** |
| `keeps` | `src/lib/keeps.ts` — a span **and** what it is like when you get there | **138** |
| `capacity`, and `costOf()`'s `standing`, `elapsed`, `longest`, `batches` | `src/lib/scaling.ts` | **46 capacities declared** |
| the branch structure — `lanes`, `criticalPath` | `src/lib/schedule.ts` | all |

**Two of those rows are the whole difficulty.** `capacity` is built and barely annotated — the
reader, the cost function and the batch arithmetic all landed, and **46 files of 685 declare one**,
so the other 639 still answer down the no-vessel branch. And **43 files of 685 carry
`keeps`, `washing-up`, `slack` and a hands-on figure that is not a guess.** That is the population a
four-field profile can rank without a hole in it.

### 3.2 The shape

A profile is three things, and it is not a score:

```
profile(occasion) = a gate  ·  a signed weighting over the fields above  ·  three answers
```

**The gate rejects.** Hard constraints that no weight can buy back.

**The weighting is signed, and the sign is the occasion's.** This is the entire mechanism of the
inversion and it needs no new field: `standing(n)` is a *cost* for the family meal and a *good* for
the dumpling party. The same number, one sign.

**The rates are in minutes-equivalent and are declared, not measured.** *A day of keeping is worth
twenty minutes back* is the occasion's statement of preference. It is not a measurement and this
file will not dress it as one. **What may never be invented is the field the rate multiplies** —
that is the repo's rule and it is untouched here.

**Three answers, not two: ranked, rejected, and cannot say.** Taken unchanged from S-010's filter
and `cooks.md`'s instrument. A recipe that never declared `keeps` is not a recipe that keeps for
zero days, and a profile that reads absence as a zero has lied in the way this whole site is built
not to.

### 3.3 Corner one — a big family meal, cooked alone

Twelve servings, one cook, a fixed hour, guests in the room. **That cook will be late**, and every
weight below follows from it.

**Gate.** `slack: unforgiving` is rejected — a dish that cannot survive being late is the wrong
dish for a cook who will be.

**Weights**, in minutes of your afternoon, lower being better:

| Term | Rate | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `standing(12)` | +1 per minute | a cost |
| `longest(12)` | +1 per minute | an unbroken run is worse than the same minutes broken up |
| `washingUp.count` | +5 per thing | the sink is the last hour |
| `keeps` | −20 per day, capped at 4 | a day of keeping is a dish made before the day |
| `slack: forgiving` | −20 | worth the most of anything here |

**Worked over eight dishes**, at twelve servings, from `buildSchedule()` and `costOf()`:

| Slug | standing | longest | wash | keeps | slack | score | what is missing |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| `chili-con-carne` | 0 | 0 | 1 | 4 days | forgiving | **−95.0** | standing is a guess |
| `smoked-turkey-breast` | 0 | 0 | — | — | unforgiving | **gated** | keeps, washing-up, standing |
| `turkey-brine` | 0 | 0 | — | — | unforgiving | **gated** | keeps, washing-up, standing |
| `cranberry-sauce` | 0 | 0 | — | — | — | 0.0 | all four |
| `mashed-potatoes` | 0 | 0 | — | — | — | 0.0 | all four |
| `baked-turkey-wings` | 0 | 0 | 1 | — | narrow | 5.0 | keeps, standing |
| `turkey-pan-gravy` | 4.5 | 4.5 | — | — | narrow | 9.0 | keeps, washing-up, standing |
| `green-beans` | 19.5 | 19.5 | — | — | — | 39.0 | all four |

**`chili-con-carne` wins the Thanksgiving profile, and it is not a Thanksgiving dish.** It wins
because it is the only one of the eight that has anything to say: four days of keeping, a forgiving
`slack`, one thing in the sink, and an elapsed time that does not move between six servings and
eighteen. Every actual holiday-plate file in this collection is silent on at least two of the four
fields the occasion is decided on.

**And the profile is right to gate the turkey, which is the interesting part.** `smoked-turkey-breast`
is `unforgiving` and that is honest — a smoked breast does not forgive a slipped hour. But it is the
centrepiece, and rejecting the centrepiece is not an answer. **The gate belongs to the plate, not to
each dish**: exactly one dish may be unforgiving, and it is the one everything else is timed around.
That is a fact about a meal rather than a recipe, and it is
[T-013-02](../active/tickets/T-013-02-why-the-meal-not-the-dish.md)'s to model. This profile can say which
dish is the risk; it cannot yet say whether the plate can afford it.

### 3.4 Corner two — a dumpling party

Twelve servings, many hands, nothing served at an hour. **Hands-on time is the point.** Every dial
above inverts.

**Gate.** `slack: unforgiving` still rejects, for a different reason: an assembly line of six people
cannot hit a narrow window.

**Weights**, higher being better:

| Term | Rate | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `standing(12)` | **+1 per minute** | the labour is the party — the sign is flipped |
| `longest(12)` | +0.5 per minute | a long forgiving assembly is what everybody sits around |
| `washingUp.count` | −2 per thing | still a cost, smaller: more hands |
| `keeps` | 0 | it is eaten tonight |
| `slack` | forgiving +20, unforgiving −40 | a party runs late by design |

**Worked over the same machinery**, at twelve servings:

| Slug | standing | longest | wash | slack | evidence | score |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |
| `gyoza` | 48 | 24 | — | narrow | inferred | **60.0** |
| `green-beans` | 19.5 | 19.5 | — | — | unknown | 29.3 |
| `samosa` | 17 | 15 | — | narrow | inferred | 24.5 |
| `egg-rolls` | 18 | 10.5 | — | narrow | inferred | 23.3 |
| `ham-sui-gok` | 8.25 | 4.5 | — | narrow | inferred | 10.5 |
| `har-gow` | 0 | 0 | — | narrow | unknown | 0.0 |

**`gyoza` first is right. Everything under it is an artefact**, and the two artefacts are different

### 3.6 What the collection did to it, and the finding that follows

**Nine of the seventeen score identically under the family profile**, because every field they would
be separated on is absent. That is not a close ranking; it is no ranking.

**Three of the four dishes a dumpling party is actually about score zero.** `har-gow`, `siu-mai` and
`xiao-long-bao` report no hands-on minutes at all, because the steps that pleat and fill them carry
no timer. [scaling.md](scaling.md) §4.3 found this on `gyoza` — *the model prices the most
scale-sensitive dish in this file as one of the cheapest* — and it is not one file's oversight. It
is the shape of the dumpling shelf. **The site systematically under-times exactly the operations an
occasion built on labour would rank by.**

**And here is the finding this section exists for.** `src/lib/schedule.ts` falls back to hands-on
when a step says nothing, and states the convention behind it: *where it errs it errs towards a
busier evening, which warns a tired cook rather than reassuring one.* That is safe **only while
hands-on time is a cost**.

**Flip the sign and the same convention starts rewarding the recipe nobody annotated.** It is not
hypothetical. `green-beans` ranks **second** in a dumpling party, above three real dumplings, on
19.5 standing minutes of which **13 were never claimed by anybody** — they are the fallback, not a
measurement.

So one rule follows, and it is the only rule in this file that constrains a future profile:

> **A profile that gives hands-on time a positive weight may score only claimed minutes** —
> `handsOnMinutes` less `assumedHandsOnMinutes` — **and must put `evidence: unknown` into cannot-say
> rather than ranking it.**

Applied to the party, the answer shrinks and becomes honest: **five of seventeen ranked, twelve
cannot say.** `gyoza` 60.0, `samosa` 24.5, `egg-rolls` 23.3, `ham-sui-gok` 10.5, `char-siu-bao` 0.0.
`green-beans` drops out entirely. So do `har-gow`, `siu-mai` and `xiao-long-bao`, which is the right
answer — the site does not know what they cost, and saying so is not the same as ranking them last.

### 3.7 Fields the profile needs and the site does not have

Named, with what each would take. Nothing beyond naming them.

| Missing | What the occasion needs it for | What it would take |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Skill floor per unit** | The party wants a job a niece can do. `slack` is about the dish's tolerance, not the person's | An authored judgement per operation, not per recipe. There is no per-step field of this kind today and no evidence in the files to derive one from |
| **Is it better with company** | The dumpling party's real question. Some dishes are no fun alone and that is why they are chosen | Authored, and unlike everything else here it is a claim about people rather than food. `keeps` is the precedent for an authored judgement that refuses to be derived |
| **What can be handed to somebody else** | The family meal's real question. `cooks.md` §3 names it as missing and says the schedule's many-hands assumption is a bug for one reader and a feature for another | Deciding how many cooks a page is talking about, then marking a task as hand-off-able. The DAG is already there; the decision about the reader is not |
| **Is it in the oven in the last hour** | The gate the family meal most needs, and cannot express | Per-step cookware. `scaling.md` §9 records that `src/generated/recipes.json` gives each step its ingredients and timers and **no vessel** — the `#oven{}` mark is flattened to one recipe-level list |
| **Can this be made the day before** | Different from `keeps`. A dish that keeps three days may still have to be assembled at five o'clock | A marker on a step rather than on a recipe. `cooks.md` records that nothing marks a step *do this the day before*, and that the existing split is attention, not calendar |
| **`capacity`, annotated** | Whether twelve servings is one pot or four loads — the difference between an afternoon and an evening | Nothing new. The machinery landed with T-011-02; the annotation pass has barely started, so 639 of 685 files still answer as though nothing binds |

## 4. One namespace, or two

`counters.md` defines a counter as a shop. **The field has already stretched once**: Instant Pot,
One Pot, The Slow Cooker and [The Air Fryer & the Pot](counters.md#the-air-fryer--the-pot) are
bargains rather than shops, and that last entry says so in its own text — *"It is also not a shop.
Nobody sells air fryer food out of a window."* An occasion in the same `>> counters:` list would be
a third kind of thing.

**For one namespace**, at its strongest. One field, one render, one front page, no schema change and
no new machinery. `>> counters:` is already a list, so a recipe can belong to a shop and a moment at
once without anything being invented — and it genuinely can: a `pan-de-muerto` is a panadería item
and a Day of the Dead item, both true, neither more true. The precedent is real and recent, and
nothing broke when the bargains went in.

**Against.** Three arguments, and the third is the one that decides it.

- **The definition stops meaning anything.** The bargains already strained *where you would get this
  if you were not making it at home*. A third kind makes the sentence decorative, and a definition
  nobody can apply becomes a judgement within a year — which is the failure The Air Fryer & the Pot
  wrote its gate down twice to prevent.
- **A counter is timeless and an occasion is not.** *Bakery* on the front page is never wrong.
  *Thanksgiving* is wrong eleven months of the year. A front page mixing Bakery, One Pot and
  Thanksgiving has stopped doing the one job it has, which is telling somebody where to go.
- **The memberships are different kinds of fact, and this is mechanical rather than aesthetic.** A
  counter's membership is an authored judgement about a shop, and it is stable — a bánh mì is a bánh
  mì next year. **An occasion's membership is `profile(occasion)` applied to measured fields**, so it
  moves: it changes when a `keeps` line is written, when a timer is added, when a `capacity` lands.
  §3.6 is the proof — the same recipes reorder entirely when one rule about unclaimed minutes
  changes. A derived, moving membership cannot live in an authored, static list. Put it there and
  the only options are to freeze it into a hand-curated list — which is the cookbook, back again —
  or to let a `counters.json` entry change silently under the reader.

**Decided: a separate axis.**

**What it costs**, stated because it is not free and because a decision without its price tag is not
a decision:

- A new field and its reader in `src/lib/`, plus a build-time check, the way `slack`, `washing-up`,
  `keeps` and `capacity` each needed one.
- `src/pages/search.json.ts` joins title, category, counters, `aka`, tags and ingredient names into
  one free-text blob. An occasion dropped into that blob becomes a word people can accidentally
  match; keeping it separate is work.
- A place to render it that is not the counter row, and an answer for what the front page shows in
  March.
- `scripts/menu-sections.mjs` round-trips `docs/gaps/**` into `src/data/counters.json` and rewrites
  every counter when it does. It would have to keep ignoring this axis, deliberately.
- If occasions are ever to be as readable as counters, a `docs/gaps/` page each — which is the real
  ongoing cost, and it is per occasion rather than one-off.

**None of that is proposed here.** It is what the answer costs.

## What could not be verified

Read this before treating anything above as settled.

**The selling pass is one pass, not six.** `counters.md` rests on six independent readings of
roughly seventy menus. This file rests on **eight searches in one sitting**, and every "somebody
sells for it" above means exactly that and no more. Where an occasion is admitted on two kinds of
evidence rather than four, the entry says so.

**The sample is biased twice**, in the same two directions `counters.md` records for menus: towards
sellers who publish online at all, and towards the United States, because the searches were run
there. Whether the same moments carry the same selling evidence elsewhere was not tested, and at
least one — the American Thanksgiving — plainly does not.

**One claim in the ticket that produced this file is wrong, and it is corrected here.** The
collection was said to carry three seasonal-board dishes: `pan-de-muerto`, mooncake, and
`hot-cross-buns`. **There is no mooncake recipe.** `recipes/custards-and-puddings/lotus-seed-paste.cook`
is the filling and names the word; no `.cook` file is a mooncake. Two of the three.

**Occasion vocabulary in this collection is close to absent**, and the numbers are small enough to
be worth stating exactly. Over 685 files, searching tags, titles, slugs and `aka`: three carry
*thanksgiving* (`cranberry-sauce`, `turkey-pan-gravy`, `turkey-brine`), three are Christmas-shaped
(`gingerbread-cookies`, `pizzelle`, `speculaas`), one is `pan-de-muerto`, one is `hot-cross-buns`,
one is a New Year dish (`chikuzenni`). The largest occasion-adjacent tag is `make-ahead`, on 50, and
`make-ahead` is not an occasion.

**Pan de muerto's window is longer and looser than the pure case wants.** It goes on sale in Mexico
from mid-September, and at least one bakery bakes it year round. The seasonal-board argument holds —
the volume is in the window — but *exists only for the moment* is a slight overstatement and is not
relied on.

**The two worked corners are a worked example, not a survey.** Seventeen recipes chosen because they
are the two corners' obvious candidates. Nothing here says what share of the collection either
occasion could be fed from — that is
[T-012-02](../active/tickets/T-012-02-what-the-shelf-offers-them.md)'s measurement and
T-013-03's to apply, and S-013 is explicit that no shelf opens until it reports.

**The weights are not measurements and never were.** *Twenty minutes for a day of keeping*, *five
minutes for a thing in the sink* — those rates are the occasion's statement of what it cares about.
They were chosen to be hand-checkable rather than tuned, and a different set would reorder the
middle of both tables. **What does not change with the rates is the inversion**, which comes from
the signs, and the silence, which comes from the collection.

**Every count here is from 7 August 2026 at 685 files and will drift.** `keeps` was 138, `slack`
416, `washing-up` 177, `capacity` 46, and the hands-on figure could speak for 269. Three of those are
being annotated on this branch by other work as this is written.

**Not looked at, so do not trust it if it appears anywhere:** whether meal trains for illness have
priced products behind them the way postpartum delivery does; regional or non-US selling evidence
for any occasion; and whether a household's own repeat purchasing — the thing that would actually
prove an occasion recurs for *them* — is visible anywhere at all.

## What this file does not do

- **It opens no occasion and no counter.** T-013-03 proves the method on two that invert; this is
  the method.
- **It proposes no field, no dial, no page and no schema.** §3.7 names what is missing and what each
  would take, and stops — `cooks.md`'s rule, and the reason a knowledge file stays useful.
- **It annotates nothing.** `cranberry-sauce` plainly keeps and is plainly the make-ahead dish of
  its plate. Writing that line is a recipe change and belongs to whoever owns the file.
- **It does not rank the occasions against each other**, and it does not say which one this
  collection should serve first.

Correct it when a rejected candidate turns out to have a product behind it, or when a profile is
held against these rules and the verdict is obviously wrong — that is this file failing, not the
profile.
