# Taquería — what is missing

**17 recipes.** The vehicles are here, the sides are here, the salsa shelf is half here — and **the
filling list, which is the actual menu, has two entries.** A taquería board is a grid: a fixed set of
filling words applied across a fixed set of vehicles. Right now the grid is two columns wide.

---

## What it has

**Fillings — the guisado and carne list.** carnitas · birria-de-res (its aka carries quesabirria and
taco de birria)

**What it goes in.** corn-tortillas (tortilla taquera) · flour-tortillas · arepas-de-queso (a real
griddle-counter item, though Venezuelan rather than Mexican)

**Arroz y frijoles.** mexican-red-rice (arroz rojo) · refried-beans (frijoles refritos) ·
cuban-black-beans · black-bean-soup

**Salsas and the table.** salsa-roja · guacamole · red-enchilada-sauce · mole-poblano

**The spice shelf.** chili-powder · taco-seasoning · mojo-marinade

**Dessert.** flan

---

## What it is missing

1. **Al pastor** (pastor, adobada, trompo) — the marquee filling of any taquería with a spit, and the
   one people specifically come looking for a recipe for.
2. **Salsa verde** — salsa roja is here alone. Every board prints the pair, and the reference lists
   them on one line for exactly that reason.
3. **Carne asada** — the default. Nothing grilled exists at this counter.
4. **Pollo asado** and **tinga de pollo** — the chicken column, empty.
5. **Chile verde pork** (puerco en salsa verde).
6. **Lengua**, **suadero**, **cachete / cabeza**, **tripa** — the four cuts that tell you it is a real
   taquería and not a burrito shop. All four are on the reference's list and none is here.
7. **Consomé** — birria is written and the cup of broth you dip it in is not, which is half the order.
8. **Cebolla y cilantro**, **salsa de aguacate**, **crema** — the garnish tray that arrives with every
   taco unpriced.
9. **Escabeche de zanahoria y jalapeño** — the cup of pickled carrots and jalapeños. **Nothing in the
   whole collection is pickled**, which lands hardest here and at the Deli.
10. **Chiles toreados** — blistered whole chiles, on the side.
11. **Quesabirria** — griddled with cheese in a fat-dipped tortilla. It is in birria's aka but it is
    its own board line and its own operations.
12. **Chile relleno** — battered, fried, and also sold as a burrito filling.
13. **Sopes**, **huaraches**, **mulitas**, **vampiros**, **gringas** — the masa vehicles beyond a
    tortilla. Five board lines, none written.
14. **Torta** and **torta cubana** — and the bolillo they sit on is missing at the Panadería too.
15. **Flautas** (taquitos, rolled tacos) — nothing deep-fried at this counter.
16. **Alambre** — chopped on the griddle with bacon, peppers and cheese.
17. **Machaca con huevo** — the breakfast line.
18. **Agua fresca: horchata**, **jamaica**, **tamarindo** — sold by the cup from a jar, and there is no
    drink recipe anywhere on the site.
19. **Elote** and **esquites** — the corn off the cart out front.
20. **Pupusa revuelta**, **curtido**, **baleada** — the pupusería block, recorded in this entry of the
    reference and unrepresented here.
21. **Salsa macha** or **salsa taquera** — the chile de árbol one, poured from a squeeze bottle.
22. **Arroz con leche** — exists on the site as rice-pudding at the Diner and Deli; it belongs on this
    board too.

## Components it would need

- **Adobo para al pastor** — guajillo, achiote and pineapple juice. The missing thing behind the
  missing thing: someone who liked al pastor is looking for this and has no name for it.
- **Toasted dried-chile purée** (guajillo and ancho, toasted, soaked, blended, strained) — birria,
  red-enchilada-sauce, mole and adobada all begin here. Four written and missing dishes share one
  table, which is the strongest argument for writing it once.
- **Salsa verde cruda** and **salsa verde asada** — raw and charred are different sauces on the same
  board.
- **Achiote / recado rojo paste**.
- **Consomé de birria** — the strained braising liquid, which cannot be split off the birria table
  (see below).
- **Escabeche**, **curtido**, **cebolla encurtida** — three pickles, zero written.
- **Masa for sopes and huaraches** — masa beaten with a little flour and salt so it holds a pinched
  rim. Different from tortilla masa.
- **Queso Oaxaca**, **queso fresco**, **crema mexicana** — quesabirria, gringas and every taco topping
  need one of these.
- **Chicharrón**, in both senses the reference records: crackling, and the Salvadoran soft ground
  paste, which are two different foods under one word.
- **Bolillo / telera** — at the Panadería, and the torta cannot exist without it.
- **Nixtamalised masa** — see the Panadería.
- **Horchata base** (soaked rice and cinnamon, blended and strained).

## What it could not stock

- **Al pastor as the trompo makes it.** A vertical spit is cooked and served in one continuous
  motion over hours: the outside is shaved off as it chars, and the stack is never finished. There is
  no final operation. A home version — marinate, stack in a loaf pan, roast, slice, crisp — is
  writable and worth writing, but it is a different dish and should say so.
- **Birria and its consomé from one table.** The braise produces two things sold separately, and one
  preparation cannot feed two endings. Either the consomé is a note on the birria table, or it is its
  own recipe that starts from the braise. It cannot be a second branch of the same tree.
- **The grid.** "Pick a filling, then pick what it goes in" is the entire menu logic, and Regular /
  Deluxe / Super is the size ladder that stands in for numbers. A permutation is not a recipe. The
  honest form is a filling table and a vehicle table joined by `pairs-with`.
- **A mojado burrito.** A finished burrito flooded with red chile sauce and melted cheese — three
  finished tables and a ladle.
- **Aguas frescas.** Blend, strain, sweeten: three ingredients and two operations, under the floor on
  both axes. Three of them on one table would be a split. They probably want one table for the
  method with the fruit named, or a single horchata table (which does earn its rows).
- **The griddle plancha.** Everything on this board finishes on a flat surface that has had pork fat on
  it since morning. The surface is the seasoning and it is not an ingredient row.
