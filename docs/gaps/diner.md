# Diner — what is missing

**43 recipes**, the second-largest counter, and it is the best-balanced one on the site: eleven soups, four
griddle items, five gravies, five blue-plate braises and a full dessert case. **What it is missing is
breakfast** — which is the section header this counter is defined by. There is no egg, no potato, no meat
choice, no biscuit and no pie.

---

## What it has

**Breakfast all day — eggs, meats, potatoes.** — nothing.

**The griddle.** buttermilk-pancakes (short stack, hotcakes) · buttermilk-waffles · crepes · dutch-baby ·
english-muffins

**Gravies and sauces.** sausage-gravy · turkey-pan-gravy · cheddar-cheese-sauce · hollandaise ·
homemade-ketchup

**Soup of the day** — the strongest section on the site. chicken-noodle-soup · new-england-clam-chowder ·
corn-chowder · cream-of-mushroom-soup · french-onion-soup · tomato-soup · potato-leek-soup ·
split-pea-soup · butternut-squash-soup · black-bean-soup · avgolemono

**Blue plates.** pot-roast (its aka carries hot beef commercial) · beef-stew · irish-stew ·
hungarian-goulash · chili-con-carne

**Sides.** cheese-grits · rice-pilaf

**Dressings, by the cup on the side.** ranch-dressing · blue-cheese-dressing · honey-mustard-dressing ·
green-goddess-dressing

**The dessert case.** rice-pudding · butterscotch-pudding · chocolate-pudding · tapioca-pudding ·
bread-pudding · french-vanilla-ice-cream · banana-bread · carrot-cake · gingerbread-cake ·
pineapple-upside-down-cake · fudgy-cocoa-brownies

**Sandwiches and burgers.** — nothing.

## What it is missing

1. **Home fries** and **hash browns** — the potato is on every plate, and the reference is explicit that the
   diced cut and the shredded cut are two separate printed lines because most places let you pick. Neither
   is here.
2. **Creamed chipped beef** (S.O.S., chipped beef on toast) — all five breakfast menus read carried it, one
   of them four ways: over toast, over a waffle, as a side, and as gravy. The most conspicuously *named*
   absence at this counter.
3. **Biscuits** — `sausage-gravy` is written with nothing under it, which is the easiest fix on this page.
4. **Corned beef hash** — chopped and pressed on the flat-top until a crust forms.
5. **French toast** — the griddle's third item; pancakes and waffles are both here.
6. **Bacon**, **sausage patties**, **ham steak**, and above all **scrapple** — the meat-choice list. Scrapple
   sits in that list as a co-equal choice, so you order it as a substitution rather than a dish, and *pon
   haus* is the kind of name nobody would know to search.
7. **Pork roll, egg & cheese** — worth writing for the `aka` alone: Taylor ham in north Jersey, pork roll in
   south Jersey, and nobody uses the other side's word.
8. **Eggs Benedict** — `hollandaise` is here with nothing under it. Second-easiest fix on the page.
9. **Western (Denver) omelette** and a plain three-egg omelette.
10. **Mashed potatoes** — `turkey-pan-gravy` is written and there is nothing to pour it on.
11. **Hot beef commercial** — `pot-roast` carries it in its aka, but the open-faced plate over white bread
    with mashed potatoes alongside is its own construction, and the turkey version is a second line.
12. **Apple pie** — and a **pie crust**, which the whole site lacks (see below).
13. **A burger**, **patty melt**, **club sandwich**, **grilled cheese**, **BLT**, **tuna melt** — the entire
    sandwich page.
14. **Chicken fried steak** with white gravy.
15. **Meatloaf** — with the same gravy as everything else.
16. **Fries** and **onion rings** — nothing on the site is deep-fried.
17. **Milkshake**, **brewed coffee**, **egg cream** — the counter, and the site has no drink at all.
18. **Hot turkey sandwich** and **tuna noodle casserole** — the rest of the blue-plate rotation.
19. **Jell-O** and **banana split** — the case's other half.
20. **Two eggs any style** — recorded below as something a table struggles with, but it is the counter's
    first printed line and it deserves a solution rather than an omission.

## Components it would need

- **Pie crust** — all-butter or shortening. **This is the most-reused missing component on the entire site**:
  apple pie, pecan pie, sweet potato pie, quiche, chicken pot pie, egg custard tart, empanada de piña, pâté
  chaud. Four counters unlock at once.
- **Biscuit dough** — sausage gravy, a chicken biscuit, strawberry shortcake, and the cobbler topping at
  Meat and Three.
- **Milk gravy** (a plain white roux gravy) — creamed chipped beef, chicken fried steak, and the reference's
  note that the same gravy goes on four different things at the same counter.
- **A blanch-then-fry potato method** — home fries, hash browns, fries. One table, three menu lines.
- **Whipped cream** — on eight case items and written nowhere.
- **Chocolate syrup**, **caramel sauce**, **hot fudge** — `french-vanilla-ice-cream` is here and the sundae
  is not.
- **A burger patty and the smash** — coarse grind, no binder, pressed hard once on a hot surface.
- **Pancake syrup / warmed maple** — what the griddle section arrives with.
- **Buttered toast** — genuinely too small for a table, and worth saying so, because half this menu sits on it.
- **Bacon fat** — the medium a third of this board is cooked in. Not an ingredient you buy; a by-product you
  keep.

---

## What it could not stock

- **Two eggs any style.** The doneness word *is* the order — over easy, over medium, over hard, sunny side
  up, basted, scrambled soft. Two ingredients and one operation puts it under the floor on both axes, and a
  single table holding all six styles would be six splits. This is a real limit and the honest answer is
  prose, or one table per style if any of them earns its rows.
- **Blue plate special.** One printed plate that changes by the day, sides included. A schedule with a price.
- **"Breakfast all day."** A policy printed as a section header, and the reference notes it is an actual
  header rather than a slogan — which makes it the counter's identity and not a dish.
- **The flat-top.** Bacon, eggs, potatoes and pancakes share one surface all day, and the surface is the
  seasoning. It cannot be an ingredient row and it is a large part of why diner food tastes like diner food.
- **A short stack.** Two pancakes instead of three so it can share the plate. Portioning.
- **Substitutions.** Scrapple instead of bacon, hash browns instead of home fries. The meat-choice list is a
  swap list, and a table cannot hold four meats without splitting the plate.
- **Bottomless coffee.** An urn and a habit.
- **The open-faced plate.** Pot roast and gravy over white bread with mashed potatoes alongside is three
  finished things arranged on a plate, and the arrangement — bread underneath, potatoes beside — is the item.
