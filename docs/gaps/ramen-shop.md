# Ramen Shop — what is missing

**10 recipes**, and **none of them is a broth.** At every other counter the top-level split is the
protein; here it is the broth, and "Tonkotsu Ramen" gets its own section with six variations inside it.
That section is empty. So is the toppings list. What is here is the shelf — dressings, a spice blend, a
curry — plus two Korean pancakes that landed on this counter and probably want their own room.

---

## What it has

**Broths — the menu's first decision.** — nothing.

**Toppings you tick off.** — nothing.

**The small fried and griddled plates.** okonomiyaki · haemul-pajeon (Korean, and one of two items here
that belong to a different board)

**Rice and donburi.** japanese-beef-curry (kare raisu)

**Soup.** miso-soup

**Custard.** chawanmushi

**The shelf.** goma-dare · miso-ginger-dressing · teriyaki-sauce · shichimi-togarashi ·
bulgogi-marinade (Korean)

## What it is missing

1. **Tonkotsu broth** — pork bones boiled hard until the collagen breaks into the water and it turns
   opaque. The counter's whole organising principle, absent.
2. **Shoyu**, **shio** and **miso ramen** — the other three broths. All four are section headers on a
   real board.
3. **Chashu** — rolled, tied, braised, chilled, sliced into rounds. It is on top of every bowl and it is
   the second thing anyone asks how to make.
4. **A bowl of ramen** — noodles, broth, tare, aroma oil, toppings. **No noodle dish exists on the
   site.**
5. **Ramen noodles** — alkaline (kansui) dough, and the reason ramen noodles behave the way they do.
6. **Ajitama** — the soy-and-mirin egg, printed as "1/2 Egg". Two ingredients and a wait, so it may sit
   under the table's floor; it is still the most-asked-about item at this counter and worth solving.
7. **Gyoza** — fried flat on one side then steamed in the same pan. Both ramen menus read had it; there
   is not a dumpling on the site.
8. **Karaage** — potato-starch fried chicken, shatteringly thin crust. Nothing fried on the site.
9. **Menma**, **kikurage**, **naruto maki**, **nori**, **corn and butter** — the toppings list, all of it.
10. **Katsudon**, **gyudon**, **oyakodon** — the donburi section, three lines, none written.
11. **Tonkatsu** — the panko cutlet. Katsudon, katsu curry and the Bakery's katsu sando all wait on it,
    and japanese-beef-curry is already here with nothing to lay across it.
12. **Tantanmen**, **tsukemen**, **mazemen** — the three variations that are not just a broth swap.
13. **Chahan** — Japanese fried rice, seasoned with soy and pepper rather than oyster sauce. Distinct
    from the Takeout Counter's.
14. **Korokke** — potato croquette with fruity brown sauce.
15. **Onigiri** — and the seasoned rice under it.
16. **Edamame**, **agedashi tofu**, **takoyaki**, **wakame salad** — the rest of the snack list.
17. **Curry udon** or **yakisoba** — the other noodle the board carries.
18. **Ramune**, **hot green tea**, **calpico** — the drink, and there is no drink on the site.

## Components it would need

- **Tare** — shoyu tare, shio tare, miso tare. **The broth is unseasoned; the tare is what makes a bowl
  "shoyu ramen".** This is the counter's version of the pâté problem: it is the thing that names the
  dish, it is never printed on a menu, and nobody knows to look it up.
- **Aroma oil** — mayu (black garlic oil), chicken fat, scallion oil. The float on top, added last,
  and the third of the three things a bowl is built from.
- **Kansui noodle dough** — alkaline water, low hydration, rested and cut.
- **Dashi** — kombu and katsuobushi. miso-soup and chawanmushi are both here and both assume it. On its
  own table it would feed a dozen items across two counters. Probably the highest-leverage component
  on this page after tare.
- **Chashu braising liquid** — which is also a tare, and therefore is exactly the split described below.
- **Panko cutlet** (tonkatsu) — flour, egg, panko, fried; the base of four missing dishes.
- **Tonkatsu sauce** — okonomiyaki is written and needs it now.
- **Okonomiyaki sauce** and **Japanese mayonnaise** — same, and neither is the same as the other.
- **Potato-starch dredge** — karaage.
- **Gyoza wrapper** and **gyoza filling**.
- **Seasoned rice** — onigiri, donburi, chahan.
- **Curry roux block** — japanese-beef-curry presumably makes its roux inline; on its own table it also
  serves katsu curry and curry udon.
- **Katsuobushi-and-mirin sweet dashi** — the simmering liquid for gyudon, oyakodon and katsudon.

## What it could not stock

- **A bowl of ramen as one table.** Five preparations — broth, tare, aroma oil, noodles, toppings — each
  its own recipe, and the bowl is assembled in ninety seconds at the pass. It is assembly, not cooking.
  Write the five, and write the bowl as a short table that consumes them.
- **A chashu braise that becomes the tare.** The braising liquid seasons the broth *and* the pork sits
  in it: one preparation, two later uses. The build refuses it, and rightly. Two tables, and a note on
  each pointing at the other.
- **Toppings A / B / C and Combo A / B / C / D.** Bundles, lettered so people order "the B". Priced
  groupings, not dishes.
- **Kaedama.** A second helping of plain noodles ordered mid-meal and dropped into the broth you have
  left. A service, and a lovely one, but there is nothing to draw.
- **Tonkotsu at the shop's scale.** Twelve to eighteen hours of hard rolling boil with water topped up
  as it goes. A table can hold `~boil{12%hr}` as one operation, but the topping-up is a loop and the
  shop never empties the pot. A home version is a one-off and the recipe should say so.
- **Noodle firmness and richness dials.** Barikata, futsu, extra fat, extra garlic. The kitchen changes
  an amount, not the dish.
- **Tsukemen's two bowls.** Noodles cool and dry in one vessel, concentrated broth in another, and they
  are deliberately never combined. Two endings, which the build forbids — and here the forbidding is
  correct, because *not merging* is the dish.
