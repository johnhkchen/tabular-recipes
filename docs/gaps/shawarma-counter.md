# Shawarma Counter — what is missing

**21 recipes**, the third-best-stocked counter on the site, and **nothing on the spit.** The dip case is
genuinely strong; the mezze list is half filled; the spice shelf is excellent. But this is the most
algorithmic board of any counter — pick one of three or four proteins, then pick a format — and the
protein column is empty, which means the top-left cell of the grid does not exist.

---

## What it has

**The spit — three or four proteins.** — nothing.

**The formats — over rice, wrap, platter, loaded fries.** — nothing.

**Cold mezze.** hummus · baba-ganoush · muhammara · tabbouleh

**Sauces.** tahini-sauce · toum · tzatziki · harissa

**Rice and grains.** rice-pilaf (vermicelli rice) · mujaddara · gigantes-plaki

**Soups.** red-lentil-soup · harira · avgolemono

**Bread.** pita-bread · injera

**The spice shelf.** zaatar · dukkah · ras-el-hanout · chermoula

**Hot.** lamb-tagine

**Shelved here from the Ethiopian board.** injera — its pair, `berbere` and `doro-wat`, landed at the
Curry House, so an Ethiopian meal is currently split across two counters. Worth a maintainer's decision;
the reference records **Ethiopian Platter** as an archetype found and deliberately not shelved.

---

## What it is missing

1. **Chicken shawarma** — the counter is named for it. Nothing on the spit at all is the defining gap here.
2. **Beef-lamb shawarma / gyro meat** — the second protein, and the Greek word for the same spit.
3. **Falafel** — the third protein and the vegetarian half of every board. Also the item whose method
   surprises people most: dried chickpeas soaked but **never cooked**, ground raw.
4. **Chicken over rice** — literally titled "1. Chicken over Rice" on one board. The single most-ordered
   line on these menus.
5. **Yellow rice** — turmeric or cumin, cooked in stock. `rice-pilaf` is here but it is not the rice that
   arrives under shaved meat, and the reference is careful that the yellow is turmeric, not saffron.
6. **Shish tawook** and **kafta** — the skewers, and the sit-down room's main lines.
7. **Labneh** — the dip case's plainest item, eaten with oil and bread, and the base under two others.
8. **Fattoush** — tabbouleh is here alone; boards print the two salads together, and fattoush's point is
   the fried pita in it.
9. **Kabis** — the plate of pickled turnip, cucumber and pepper. The pink is beet. **Nothing on the site
   is pickled.**
10. **Batata harra** — fried potato tossed off the heat with garlic, cilantro and chile.
11. **Ful medames** — the breakfast bean, dressed at the table.
12. **Kibbeh** — raw as a spread, baked flat in a tray, or fried into torpedoes. Three dishes, one word.
13. **Fatayer**, **sambousek**, **lahm bi ajeen** (sfiha), **manakish** — the bakery half of the mezze
    list. `zaatar` is written with nothing to spread it on but pita.
14. **Baklava** and **maamoul** — the sweets, and there is no filo anywhere on the site.
15. **Halloumi** and **saganaki** — the fried cheese, which is a whole category here.
16. **Horiatiki** (Greek salad, no lettuce) — and the reference notes Greek boards keep their own
    vocabulary rather than borrowing the Levantine one.
17. **Melitzanosalata**, **taramosalata**, **tirokafteri**, **fava** — the Greek dip set, of which none is
    here. `baba-ganoush` is not melitzanosalata; the difference is the tahini, and that is exactly the
    kind of distinction the site exists for.
18. **Spanakopita** and **loukoumades** — the two most-recognised Greek items after gyro.
19. **Ezme**, **haydari**, **zaalouk** — Turkish and Moroccan, one each.
20. **Mast o sir**, **makdous**, **makanek**, **soujouk**, **hummus fatteh** — the rest of the case.
21. **Loaded fries** — the fourth format, and increasingly the most-ordered.
22. **Mint tea**, **ayran** or **jallab** — drinks.

## Components it would need

- **Shawarma spice rub** — the marinade under the stack. It is the reason a home version tastes almost
  right and not right, and it does not have a cookbook name.
- **Yogurt-lemon-garlic marinade** — shish tawook, and the tenderising that makes it work.
- **Falafel mix** — soaked raw chickpeas, herbs, spice, ground and rested. Green inside. The Egyptian
  fava version (ta'ameya) is greener still and is a second table.
- **Yellow rice** — see above.
- **Labneh** — yogurt hung to drain. It is a component *and* a menu item, and it sits under tzatziki,
  haydari and mast o sir.
- **The white sauce a cart actually pours** — mayonnaise and yogurt loosened with vinegar and herbs.
  `tahini-sauce` and `tzatziki` are both here and neither is it, and the reference notes menus label it
  "tzatziki" even when there is no cucumber in it.
- **The thin red hot sauce** — `harissa` is here as the North African paste; the cart's fierce
  few-drops-at-a-time sauce is a different thing.
- **Amba** — the mango pickle sauce, on Iraqi and Israeli boards.
- **Sumac-onion garnish** — on every plate off the spit.
- **Filo handling and clarified butter** — baklava, spanakopita, fatayer.
- **Sugar syrup** (attar) — baklava, maamoul, loukoumades. Poured hot over cold or cold over hot, and
  which way round matters.
- **Kibbeh dough** — fine bulgur ground with meat until it becomes a paste.
- **Semolina maamoul dough** and **date paste**.
- **Turnip-and-beet pickling brine**.
- **Pomegranate molasses** — muhammara, fattoush and ezme all assume it and none of them can say so.
- **Toum** is already here, which is good: it is the one component people specifically hunt for.

## What it could not stock

- **The spit.** A metre of stacked marinated slices cooked and shaved continuously over six hours: the
  outside is served while the inside is still raw, and the stack never finishes. **No final operation.**
  Home versions — marinate and roast a rolled loaf, or grill and slice thin — are writable and worth
  writing, and they are a different dish.
- **The grid.** Three or four proteins × four formats × two sauces is the whole board. "Chicken over
  rice" is three finished tables and a scoop. That is the reference's own description of the counter and
  a table cannot express a permutation.
- **Platters and loaded fries.** Assembly on a tray.
- **Mezze as a meal.** The reference says it flatly: **mezze is a form, not a place.** Eight small plates
  arriving together is eight tables and a table setting.
- **A dip case with a dozen hummus flavours.** One table plus a list of stir-ins. Holding twelve on one
  table is a split; the case is the shop's, not the recipe's.
- **Makdous.** Small eggplants stuffed with walnut and garlic and kept submerged in oil for weeks. The
  operation is waiting, with a spoilage risk, and it converges to a jar rather than a dish.
- **Combo Plate.** "Two of the proteins over the same rice, because nobody wants to choose." The most
  ordered line on these boards, and it is a choice, not a recipe.
