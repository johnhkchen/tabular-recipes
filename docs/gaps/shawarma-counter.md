# Shawarma Counter — what is missing

**44 recipes, 36 of them only here.** The spit has four things on it, the mezze case is full on both
its cold and its bakery halves, and the rice, the sauces and the pickles under them are written.

What is missing is **the Greek half of the board** — halloumi, horiatiki, spanakopita,
melitzanosalata — and the platter format itself: `shish-tawook` and `yellow-rice` are both written and
nothing puts them on one plate with the white sauce over it.

---

## What it has

**The spit.** chicken-shawarma · gyro-meat · shish-tawook · kafta

**Cold mezze.** hummus · baba-ganoush · muhammara · tabbouleh · labneh · fattoush · kabis · sumac-onions

**Sauces.** tahini-sauce · toum · tzatziki · harissa · white-sauce · pomegranate-molasses

**Rice and grains.** rice-pilaf · mujaddara · gigantes-plaki · yellow-rice

**Soups.** red-lentil-soup · harira · avgolemono

**Bread.** pita-bread · manakish

**The spice shelf.** zaatar · dukkah · ras-el-hanout · chermoula · shawarma-spice

**Hot.** lamb-tagine · falafel · kibbeh · batata-harra · sambousek · fatayer · lahm-bi-ajeen · ful-medames

**Sweets.** baklava · maamoul · attar

**Shelved here from the Ethiopian board.** injera

## What it is missing

1. **Chicken over rice** — literally titled "1. Chicken over Rice" on one board. The single most-ordered
   line on these menus.

2. **Halloumi** and **saganaki** — the fried cheese, which is a whole category here.

3. **Horiatiki** (Greek salad, no lettuce) — and the reference notes Greek boards keep their own
    vocabulary rather than borrowing the Levantine one.

4. **Melitzanosalata**, **taramosalata**, **tirokafteri**, **fava** — the Greek dip set, of which none is
    here. `baba-ganoush` is not melitzanosalata; the difference is the tahini, and that is exactly the
    kind of distinction the site exists for.

5. **Spanakopita** and **loukoumades** — the two most-recognised Greek items after gyro.

6. **Ezme**, **haydari**, **zaalouk** — Turkish and Moroccan, one each.

7. **Mast o sir**, **makdous**, **makanek**, **soujouk**, **hummus fatteh** — the rest of the case.

8. **Loaded fries** — the fourth format, and increasingly the most-ordered.

9. **Mint tea**, **ayran** or **jallab** — drinks.

---

## Components it would need

- **Shawarma spice rub** — the marinade under the stack. It is the reason a home version tastes almost
  right and not right, and it does not have a cookbook name.
- **Yogurt-lemon-garlic marinade** — shish tawook, and the tenderising that makes it work.
- **Falafel mix** — soaked raw chickpeas, herbs, spice, ground and rested. Green inside. The Egyptian
  fava version (ta'ameya) is greener still and is a second table.
- **The white sauce a cart actually pours** — mayonnaise and yogurt loosened with vinegar and herbs.
  `tahini-sauce` and `tzatziki` are both here and neither is it, and the reference notes menus label it
  "tzatziki" even when there is no cucumber in it.
- **The thin red hot sauce** — `harissa` is here as the North African paste; the cart's fierce
  few-drops-at-a-time sauce is a different thing.
- **Amba** — the mango pickle sauce, on Iraqi and Israeli boards.
- **Filo handling and clarified butter** — baklava, spanakopita, fatayer.
- **Kibbeh dough** — fine bulgur ground with meat until it becomes a paste.
- **Semolina maamoul dough** and **date paste**.
- **Turnip-and-beet pickling brine**.

---

## What it could not stock

- **The spit.** A metre of stacked marinated slices cooked and shaved continuously over six hours: the
  outside is served while the inside is still raw, and the stack never finishes. **No final operation.**
  Home versions — marinate and roast a rolled loaf, or grill and slice thin — are writable and worth
  writing, and they are a different dish.
- **The grid.** Three or four proteins × four formats × two sauces is the whole board. "Chicken over
  rice" is three finished tables and a scoop. That is the reference's own description of the counter and
  a table cannot express a permutation.
- **Platters and loaded fries.** Assembly on a tray.
- **Mezze as a meal.** The reference says it flatly: **mezze is a form, not a place.** Eight small plates
  arriving together is eight tables and a table setting.
- **A dip case with a dozen hummus flavours.** One table plus a list of stir-ins. Holding twelve on one
  table is a split; the case is the shop's, not the recipe's.
- **Makdous.** Small eggplants stuffed with walnut and garlic and kept submerged in oil for weeks. The
  operation is waiting, with a spoilage risk, and it converges to a jar rather than a dish.
- **Combo Plate.** "Two of the proteins over the same rice, because nobody wants to choose." The most
  ordered line on these boards, and it is a choice, not a recipe.
