# The Bowl Shop — what is missing

**103 recipes.** T-002-05 wrote twelve grain bowls, T-002-06 twelve leafy and composed salads,
T-002-07 six proteins and six roasted vegetables; T-002-08 shelved sixty-seven that were already
here — the drawer of dressings above all, which is the fullest on the site and had never been on
a board a person could browse.

The vocabulary is read off real boards: Goop Kitchen (the reference the brief names), Sweetgreen,
Cava and Dig. Their build order is the same everywhere and it is the order of the sections here —
**base, then greens, then what goes on top, then the dressing last.**

## What it is missing

Fifteen of the twenty-two absences this page ranked have been written. Seven are still out,
renumbered, most conspicuous absence first. Named the way the boards name them.

1. **Quinoa**, **farro** and **wild rice** as plain grain tables — the three bases every one of
   these boards offers. `harvest-bowl`, `harissa-chicken-bowl`, `chicken-pesto-bowl`,
   `bbq-tofu-bowl` and `crispy-chickpea-bowl` all cook one of them inline, so the collection now
   has the ratios five times over and the plain table nowhere. This is the largest remaining hole
   and it is a factoring job, not a research one.

2. **Pickled red onions** — the default pink topping on every bowl board in the country.
   `sumac-onions` is raw and dressed; `do-chua` is carrot and daikon; `kabis` is turnip. This one
   is still missing.

3. **Whipped feta** (Cava's *Crazy Feta*) — the signature dip of the archetype, and the site has
   feta in `greek-salad` and nowhere else as the dish itself.

4. **Lemon herb tahini** and **Greek vinaigrette** — Cava names both. `tahini-sauce` is the
   Levantine one and `basic-vinaigrette` is the neutral one; these are the two the board prints.

5. **Sesame kale** — Goop's teriyaki bowl component. `kale-caesar` and `harvest-chopped-salad`
   both massage kale now, so the technique is here and this application is not.

6. **Toasted seeds and nuts** (pepitas, sunflower, candied pecans) — the crunch line.
   `harvest-chopped-salad` candies pecans inside itself and `dukkah` is a spice blend; nothing
   makes the plain toasted seed.

7. **A hot grain bowl base**, warm rather than cold — the difference between a "warm bowl" and a
   salad on these boards, and it is a technique note as much as a recipe.

## Components it would need

- **A roasting method for hard vegetables** — one hot sheet, one fat, one salt, and the doneness
  cue per vegetable. T-002-07 answered this with six files instead of one, on the ground that what
  differs per vegetable *is* the recipe; read its `design.md` §3 before reopening the argument.
- **Cooked quinoa, farro and wild rice**, as three plain grain tables. Still the largest hole:
  five of the twelve grain bowls cook one of them inline, so the ratios exist five times and the
  plain table nowhere.
- **A whole-grain cooking ratio note** — the pilaf method in `rice-pilaf` does not transfer to
  farro or wild rice, and every grain bowl above quietly assumes it does.
- **Massaged kale** — done inside `kale-caesar` and `harvest-chopped-salad`; the same factoring
  argument as the grains applies.
- **Pickled red onion** in a quick brine (not the sumac dressing, not `do-chua`).
- **A plain roasted or poached chicken breast, pulled** — written as `pulled-roast-chicken`.
  `chicken-salad`, `harvest-bowl` and the chopped salads should be repointed at it, which is a
  `pairs-with:` job for whoever reads the whole collection next.
- **A basic roasted fish fillet** — `blackened-salmon` is the cooked-fish technique now; a plain
  herb-roasted one is still worth having.
- **Balsamic vinaigrette** and **carrot-ginger dressing** — two named board dressings the drawer
  does not yet hold, despite holding forty others.

## What it could not stock

- **The bowl itself.** A bowl is an assembly: a base, a protein, three sides, a dressing. Every
  one of those is a table already, and the bowl is one operation over eight leaves — under the
  floor on operations and over it on the point, the same finding the Deli recorded for a sandwich.
  The honest form is components plus `pairs-with`, and the counter page *is* the bowl.
- **"Choose any three."** The board's real grammar is a build: base, protein, up to three sides,
  dressing, unlimited toppings. That is a combinatorial menu, not a recipe, and a table cannot
  hold a choice.
- **Free toppings.** Cava's toppings and dressings are unlimited and unpriced, which is a pricing
  model doing menu work. It shapes what people order and cannot be written down as a step.
- **Seasonal by definition.** Dig's menu is "curated by season, ingredients vary"; Sweetgreen
  rotates limited-time bowls. A recipe that says "whatever is good this week" is not a recipe, and
  a recipe that pins the vegetable has lost what the counter is for.
- **Certified-clean and allergen claims.** Goop's whole board runs on gluten-free and
  "no processed sugars, no dairy, no peanuts". That is a supply claim about ingredients bought,
  not an operation a cook performs.
- **Portioning by weight into a bowl.** The line cook's scoop is the item — the same problem the
  Deli recorded for the by-the-pound case.
- **A salad spinner's worth of greens.** Washing and drying leaves for a shop is a step nobody
  writes and everybody does, and at a table it is one cell that says "wash and dry well" and
  teaches nothing.

## What came off the dressing drawer, and why

`recipes/dressings-and-dips/` holds forty files. Twenty-four are on the board above. The test was
the counter's own build order — **would this be the last thing ladled over a finished bowl?** —
and sixteen answered no.

- **A deli spread for bread, not a drizzle over a bowl.** `chopped-liver` · `cream-cheese` ·
  `scallion-schmear` · `pork-liver-pate`. The ticket names the first two itself.
- **A topping rather than a dressing, and shelved as one.** `guacamole` · `labneh` · `paneer` ·
  `queso-fresco` · `birista` · `do-chua` · `sour-dill-pickles`. Six of these seven are on *What
  goes on top* above; being off the dressing list is a placement, not a rejection.
- **A dressed salad, and a deli-case one.** `coleslaw` · `barbecue-slaw`.
- **A preserve eaten with curry, not over greens.** `lime-pickle` · `mango-chutney`.
- **An ingredient, not a line on a board.** `mayonnaise` — it is inside `aioli`,
  `ranch-dressing`, `russian-dressing`, `green-goddess-dressing`, `blue-cheese-dressing` and
  `honey-mustard-dressing`, all of which are on the board.

The six deli-case salads went the same way for the same kind of reason: `potato-salad`,
`macaroni-salad`, `chicken-salad`, `egg-salad`, `tuna-salad` and `whitefish-salad` are sold by the
pound at the Deli, and the story opens by naming that shape as the thing this counter exists to
contrast with.

*Roasted vegetables* took only `batata-harra` from the earlier list. `green-beans`,
`stewed-squash`, `creamed-corn`, `mashed-potatoes`, `collard-greens`, `fried-okra` and
`ratatouille` are hot vegetable sides but none of them is roasted, and `candied-yams` is a dessert
with a vegetable in it — T-002-07 flagged that one itself, on the ground that shelving it would
make the section look filled when it is not. That argument applies to all eight.
