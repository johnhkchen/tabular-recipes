# Instant Pot — what is missing

**0 recipes. The shelf was opened by T-002-01 and nothing is on it yet.** This is not a cuisine
and not a storefront: it is a lid. Everything it will ever sell is already written here as a pot
on a stove, and the job is the second table — the same dish, `>> kit: Instant Pot`, with the
afternoon taken out of it.

So the ranked list below is **not a wish list**. Every line names a recipe that already exists,
with its slug, ranked by how much the pot actually helps — which is the length of the *wet* cook
it collapses, not the length of the recipe. A dish whose long wait is a marinade, a cure, a rise
or a chill gains nothing from a pressure cooker and is not on the list, however long it takes.

Ranks 1 to 12 are the ones worth writing first: each is two or three hours of covered simmer that
comes back in forty minutes. Ranks 13 to 31 are real but smaller wins. Everything after that is
the pot showing off.

The measurements come from `src/generated/recipes.json` — the longest unattended simmer, braise,
stew or poach timer in each file.

## What it is missing

Ranked by how much the pot helps. The minutes in brackets are the covered wet cook the pot
replaces, taken from the existing file.

### The twelve that pay for the appliance

1. **Tonkotsu broth** — `tonkotsu-broth` [8 hr of hard rolling boil]. The longest wet cook on the
   site and the one that most wants a sealed pot: eight hours of a boil that has to stay violent
   to emulsify, in a kitchen that smells of it all day. Ninety minutes under pressure.

2. **Phở broth** — `pho-broth` [6 hr]. Charred onion and ginger, a spice sachet, and a simmer that
   must never cloud. The pot's steadier heat is an argument on quality, not only on time.

3. **Birria de res** — `birria-de-res` [3 to 4 hr in a 325°F oven]. Also feeds
   `consome-de-birria`, which is the same pot's liquid, so one variant closes two lines.

4. **Chintan broth** — `chintan-broth` [4 hr]. The clear one. A pressure cooker holds a bare
   quiver better than a burner does, which is the whole difficulty of a clear broth.

5. **Carnitas** — `carnitas` [3 hr at 300°F]. The confit-and-crisp shape splits cleanly: pressure
   for the tender, then the broiler leg unchanged.

6. **Pot roast** — `pot-roast` [3 hr]. The dish the appliance is sold on, and the one a person
   most often buys the appliance for.

7. **Braised short ribs** — `braised-short-ribs` [3 hr]. Collagen, wine, a sealed vessel. Nothing
   about it resists the pot.

8. **Chāshū** — `chashu` [3 hr]. Rolled belly in a soy braise, and the ramen shelf's slowest
   component.

9. **Clear chicken broth** — `chicken-broth` [3 hr]. The Deli's, and the base of four soups.

10. **Ox tails** — `oxtails` [3 hr]. All collagen; the pot's best case.

11. **Cachete** — `cachete` [3 hr]. Beef cheek, same argument, and the taquería wants it weekly
    rather than on a Sunday.

12. **Beef bourguignon** — `beef-bourguignon` [3 hr]. With the reduction left on the stove, where
    it belongs — see *What it could not stock*.

## Components it would need

- **A pressure-braise timing table.** Not a recipe: the one piece of shared knowledge the whole
  shelf rests on. Chuck at 35 minutes, short rib at 40, tongue at 45, oxtail at 45, pork shoulder
  at 45, chicken thigh at 12, dried chickpeas at 35 from dry, black beans at 25, pinto at 25.
  Without it every writer re-derives the same numbers and they will not agree.
- **A note on natural versus quick release**, and which dishes are ruined by the wrong one. Beans
  split under a quick release; a whole bird tightens. This is a two-line convention that belongs
  somewhere central, not in twenty tables.
- **The sauté-then-seal grammar.** Almost every dish on the list starts with a brown, and the pot
  does it in the same vessel. That is the *whole* claim of the appliance and it needs to be one
  operation in the table, not a footnote.
- **A reduction step to hand back.** Pressure adds no evaporation, so nearly every braise comes
  out looser than the plain version. Every variant ends with the lid off, and it should be the
  same operation everywhere.
- **`~pressure cook`, `~natural release`, `~quick release` and `~come to pressure` as timer
  names** — added to `src/lib/time.ts` by T-002-01, so the timeline already reads them as time you
  are not there for. Writers should name their timers with these exact words.

## What it could not stock

- **The reduction.** A pressure cooker is a sealed vessel and nothing evaporates in it. Any dish
  whose finish is "reduce until it coats" — `beef-bourguignon`, `red-braised-pork-belly`,
  `beef-rendang`, `char-siu` — cannot be one sealed operation. The table can hold pressure *then*
  reduce; it cannot hold a braise that thickens as it cooks.
- **Anything judged by looking.** "Until it falls off the bone" is the instruction in half the
  braises here, and under a locked lid you cannot look. The pot trades a judgement for a number,
  and a table that pretends otherwise is lying to a cook who opens it early.
- **A cure, a marinade, a rise or a ferment.** `pastrami` waits five days, `sauerkraut` twenty-
  three, `falafel` soaks overnight, `vindaloo` marinates twelve hours. None of that is pressure's
  to give back. Those recipes appear above only for the simmer at the end.
- **Browning at volume.** The pot's sauté function is a small element under a narrow base, and
  three pounds of beef browns in five batches. The plain version's skillet is genuinely better,
  and the honest variant says so rather than claiming one vessel.
- **Anything that needs to stay separate.** `biryani` is layered and steamed sealed so the rice
  stays in grains; pressure gives it one texture. Rice cooks *well* in the pot but not
  *differently*, which is why the grain section here is short and always will be.
- **The pot as the dish.** A tagine, a cazuela, a Dutch oven and a bean pot are named on the menu
  because the vessel is part of what is being sold. Swapping in an appliance is a real change to
  `lamb-tagine`, `suadero` and `boston-baked-beans`, and the `>> kit:` line is the right place to
  say so — not a silent substitution.
