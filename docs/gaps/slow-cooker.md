# The Slow Cooker — what is missing

**0 recipes. The shelf was opened by T-003-01 and nothing is on it yet.** Like the Instant Pot it
is a lid, not a cuisine, and everything it will ever sell is already written here as a pot on a
stove. Unlike the Instant Pot, it is the *second* lid: **25 Instant Pot variants already exist**,
so for most of the dishes below a cook can already see two ways to spend the day and this shelf
adds the third.

That is the whole point of the counter. A person looking at `pot-roast` should be able to read:

```
(plain)              4 hr 30, tended
kit: Instant Pot       50 min, unattended
kit: Slow Cooker         8 hr, unattended and gone
```

So the ranked list below is **not a wish list**, and it is not a copy of `instant-pot.md`'s
either. Every line names a recipe that already exists, with its slug, and every line carries a
verdict: whether the machine helps **more**, **less**, or **differently** than pressure does for
that dish. A shelf of eighteen dishes the machine genuinely improves is worth more than thirty
where a third of them come out worse than the original. Read this page beside
`docs/gaps/instant-pot.md`; the two were written to be read together.

**The times are not on this page.** A slow-cooker time is not a braise time doubled, and this file
will not invent one. What it gives is the setting the dish wants and the reason; T-003-05 sources
the hours per dish and records where each came from.

## What it is missing

### Read it in this order

**Write the twelve below first, in order, then the six after them.** The first twelve all name a
dish that already has an Instant Pot variant, which is what makes the three-way choice visible on
the page — and it is T-003-05's criterion that at least twelve do. The six after are dishes the
machine is *better* at than the pressure cooker but which have no pressure variant to sit beside.
Taking twelve from the first block and six from the second satisfies both counts without anybody
having to work it out.

### The twelve that pay for the appliance — and already have a pressure sibling

1. **Pot roast** — `pot-roast` [Instant Pot variant exists]. The dish the machine is sold on, in
   both directions. Chuck, a low setting and a day away. The three-way page starts here.

2. **Chili con carne** — `chili-con-carne` [IP]. The slow cooker's signature dish in American
   home cooking, and our version carries **no beans at all**, so the one hazard of a slow-cooked
   chili does not apply. A long low chili tastes rounder than a fast one; this is a *more*.

3. **Carnitas** — `carnitas` [IP]. Confit-and-crisp splits the same way it does under pressure:
   the machine does the tender, the broiler leg is unchanged. Say in the table that the broiler is
   not optional.

4. **Corned beef** — `corned-beef` [IP]. A boiled dinner wants a bare simmer it cannot overshoot,
   and pressure will shred the brisket before the vegetables are done. The five-day cure stays a
   five-day cure; only the simmer moves.

5. **Birria de res** — `birria-de-res` [IP]. Also feeds `consome-de-birria`, which is the same
   pot's liquid, so one variant closes two lines — the same argument `instant-pot.md` makes at
   its rank 3.

6. **Cachete** — `cachete` [IP]. Beef cheek is pure collagen and almost impossible to overshoot in
   a machine that cannot exceed a simmer. Forty minutes of pressure can overshoot it; eight hours
   of low cannot.

7. **Ox tails** — `oxtails` [IP]. Same argument, plus the practical one: the fat cap sets hard
   overnight and lifts off in a sheet, which the pressure version has no time to allow.

8. **Braised short ribs** — `braised-short-ribs` [IP]. Collagen, wine, a sealed vessel. The one
   thing to say honestly is that the sauce comes out looser than the plain version and has to be
   thickened deliberately at the end.

9. **Beef stew** — `beef-stew` [IP]. The weeknight case. Potatoes and carrots that go in at the
   start will be soft; whether that is a fault or the dish is a real argument and the table should
   take a side.

10. **Chile verde** — `chile-verde` [IP]. Tomatillos break down to the sauce on their own, so it
    is a genuine one-step-in dish with no reduction to hand back.

11. **Collard greens** — `collard-greens` [IP]. All day with a hock is how these are actually
    cooked, and the machine is the vessel that matches the method rather than shortening it. Pot
    likker included.

12. **Boston baked beans** — `boston-baked-beans` [IP]. The one bean dish where slow beats
    pressure outright: `instant-pot.md` says at its own rank 31 that the long oven bake is doing
    flavour work pressure does not reproduce. A crock is the closest vessel to a bean pot there
    is. **Read the bean hazard below before writing it.**

### Six more the machine is better at, with no pressure sibling to sit beside

13. **Osso buco** — `osso-buco`. The shins have to hold their shape on the bone.
    `instant-pot.md` flags exactly this as the risk its own variant runs; the slow cooker does not
    run it.

14. **Lamb tagine** — `lamb-tagine`. A tagine is a slow vessel by definition. Swapping in a slow
    cooker is a substitution a cook will forgive; swapping in a pressure cooker is a different
    dish. The `>> kit:` line is where to say so.

15. **Irish stew** — `irish-stew`. Lamb, potato and onion, nothing to brown, everything in at
    once. This is the shape the machine was built for and it is the easiest table on the list.

16. **New England boiled dinner** — `new-england-boiled-dinner`. The vegetables go in on a
    staggered clock — that is the whole method, and it is the one thing a locked pressure lid
    cannot do.

17. **Brunswick stew** — `brunswick-stew`. Long, forgiving, and better the next day.

18. **Soy sauce chicken** — `soy-sauce-chicken`. A master-stock poach wants a liquid that never
    boils. A slow cooker on low is closer to that than a burner is, and much closer than pressure,
    which tightens a whole bird.

## Components it would need

- **A setting-and-hours table, sourced.** Low and high are not the same recipe at two speeds. A
  Crock-Pot on low takes **7–8 hours** to reach its simmer point of about 209°F and on high takes
  **3–4 hours** ([Crockpot support](https://www.crock-pot.com/on/demandware.store/Sites-crockpot-Site/default/Support-Show?cfid=help-and-how-to-use-cook-time-and-quantity-faq)),
  which is why a dish written for low and cooked on high is a different dish. Without one shared
  table every writer re-derives these and they will not agree.
- **A liquid rule.** Nothing evaporates. The manufacturer's own guidance is to cut a stovetop or
  oven recipe's liquid by **25–50 %**. That is a change to every ingredient row on the list and it
  cannot be left to each writer's judgement.
- **A thickening step to hand back.** Every braise here comes out looser than the plain version.
  A slurry stirred in and held on high for 15–30 minutes, or the liquid pulled into a pan — one
  operation, the same everywhere ([ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/7175-how-to-thicken-thin-or-watery-slow-cooker-sauces)).
- **The brown-in-a-pan operation.** The machine cannot brown. Either the recipe browns in a
  skillet as its own operation and says so, or it is honestly a dish that does not need it — and
  says *that*. Two of the eighteen above (`irish-stew`, `soy-sauce-chicken`) genuinely do not.
- **A late-additions convention.** Dairy, delicate herbs and seafood go in at the end. Eight hours
  is not a longer version of forty minutes for anything delicate.
- **Timer names the clock already reads.** `~slow cook` is already in the unattended vocabulary in
  `src/lib/time.ts`; T-002-01 added the pressure words beside it. Writers should check whether
  `~keep warm` and `~on low` read as unattended before relying on them, and say so if they do not.

### The bean hazard, which is a safety fact and not trivia

Dried **red kidney beans** contain phytohaemagglutinin, and a slow cooker on low may never get hot
enough to destroy it; the guidance is to soak, discard the water and **boil the beans hard in
fresh water for at least 10 minutes** before they go anywhere near the machine
([Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/live-well-eat-well/2024-02-29-kidney-beans-and-slow-cookers),
[K-State](https://www.johnson.k-state.edu/programs/health-food-safety/newsletter-articles/agents-articles/do-not-cook-dry-beans-in-slow-cooker.html)).

Checked against the whole collection: **no recipe here uses kidney beans.** The dried beans on the
shelf are black, pinto, navy, lima, gigante and black-eyed, and `chili-con-carne` carries no beans
at all. So this bites nothing that exists — it is a constraint on what T-003-05 may write, and the
reason `boston-baked-beans` (navy) should still say plainly that the beans are boiled first.

## What it could not stock

- **Tonkotsu broth.** `tonkotsu-broth` needs eight hours of a boil violent enough to emulsify fat
  and collagen into the water. A slow cooker's whole design is that it cannot boil hard. This is
  not a worse version, it is a different liquid, and it is the one dish on the stock list that
  should be refused outright rather than written badly.
- **Beef rendang.** `beef-rendang` is finished by cooking the coconut liquid away until the meat
  fries in the oil that breaks out of it. Nothing cooks away under a slow lid. The same objection
  retires `char-siu` and softens `red-braised-pork-belly` to a braise-then-reduce.
- **Anything smoked.** `chopped-pork`, `smoked-brisket`, `burnt-ends`, `pastrami`'s smoke leg. A
  slow cooker is a moist sealed vessel and smoke is the dish. The machine can hold pulled pork
  warm; it cannot make it.
- **A crust, a sear or a crisp.** Nothing browns at 209°F under a lid full of steam. Every dish
  above that wants colour gets it in a pan before or a broiler after, and the table has to show
  that operation rather than imply it.
- **Anything judged by looking.** The same limit as the Instant Pot for the opposite reason: you
  *can* open a slow cooker, but every lift costs about twenty minutes of recovery, so a recipe
  that says "check it every half hour" has thrown the day away it was supposed to save.
- **A short cook stretched.** `white-cut-chicken`, `smothered-pork-chops`, `coq-au-vin`,
  `egg-drop-soup` — under an hour on the stove, and eight hours does not improve any of them. The
  shelf's promise is a day you get back, not a longer cook.
- **The claim that the machine is unattended when the recipe is not.** A dish that browns in a
  pan, then slow-cooks, then thickens on the stove is three sessions in a kitchen. The table shows
  every operation, so the shelf is honest by construction — but the writer should not call
  something "leave it and go" when the cook has to be home at both ends.
