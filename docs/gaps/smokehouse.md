# Smokehouse — what is missing

**21 recipes, 14 of them only here.** The pit has eight things on it, both sauces are written, and the
old header's two complaints are gone: there is cornbread — two of them — and there is a hushpuppy.

What is missing is **the regional sauce set** and one meat: Alabama white, Carolina mustard and Kansas
City thick are three tables that each unlock a printed line, and there is no smoked sausage. The
brisket carries its salt-and-pepper rub inline, which is the whole of that rub; a separate file would
be one ingredient row and a whisk.

## What it is missing

1. **Smoked sausage / hot links**.

2. **Pecan pie** — the rest of the dessert list. `peach-cobbler` is written; this one waits on a pie
   shell being consumed rather than re-derived.

3. **Sweet tea** — the drink. Three are written and not one of them is brewed and sweetened.

4. **Mac and cheese** — `macaroni-and-cheese` is written and is shelved at Meat and Three only. Whether
   it should also print here is a board judgement rather than an edit; both counters sell it.

## Components it would need

- **Alabama white sauce**, **Carolina mustard sauce**, **Kansas City thick sauce** — one sauce is written and
  **the regional split is the entire story of this counter.** Three short tables would say more about
  barbecue than any single recipe could.
- **Cornbread batter** — baked in a hot greased skillet so the edge fries. Also needed at Meat and Three.
- **Hushpuppy batter** — cornmeal, a little sugar or not depending on the state, dropped by the spoon.
- **A beef rub** — salt and coarse pepper, and nothing else. `memphis-dry-rub` is paprika and brown sugar, and
  the fact that brisket does *not* get that rub is the whole point of having both.
- **A mop or spritz** — vinegar and juice, applied through the cook. It is a component that gets used ten
  times over twelve hours, which is a shape a table handles poorly and prose handles well.
- **Rendered pit drippings / beef tallow** — for the brisket wrap and for seasoning the beans.
- **Slaw dressing**, both ways — mayonnaise and vinegar — since the two slaws are the same cabbage.
- **Vanilla custard and wafer layers** — banana pudding, and the Diner's case would take it too.

## What it could not stock

- **The pit.** A brisket is twelve to sixteen hours at 250°F, wrapped partway, in a fire someone is tending
  the whole time. A table can hold `~smoke{12%hr}` as one operation — and should — but fire management is the
  craft and an offset smoker is the equipment. Written for an oven or a kettle the recipe is honest and it is
  a different piece of meat; the file should say which.
- **Meat by the pound.** The board sells weight, not dishes. "A pound of chopped, a pint of slaw, six
  hushpuppies" is a basket someone hands you.
- **Tray versus Plate.** At North Carolina pits these are two named *combinations* rather than two sizes — a
  tray is meat with slaw and bread, a plate brings fries or hushpuppies into it — and which holds more meat
  varies by pit. Assembly plus a local convention.
- **A whole hog.** Some pits cook one animal and four menu items come off it: shoulder, ribs, the outside
  brown, the rest. One preparation yielding four products is the definitive split, and the build refuses it
  for good reason.
- **Sauce on the table.** The dip is not in the dish. It is poured by the eater onto the meat, the bread and
  the fries alike, which makes it a `pairs-with` rather than a step.
- **Bark.** The dark crust is a result of hours, airflow and rendered fat, and "coarse chopped" means keeping
  more of it. You cannot make bark a row, and the recipe can only say not to trim it off.
- **Smoke as an ingredient.** Wood species is a real variable — hickory, oak, post oak, cherry — but it is
  fuel, not an ingredient, and it goes in the cookware column at best.
