# Bakery — what is missing

**107 recipes, 63 of them only here** — the biggest counter on the site by a wide margin, and the one
whose case is now genuinely full. The pastry shell is written twice over (`all-butter-pie-crust`,
`sweet-tart-shell`), the laminated dough three times (`hojaldre`, `croissant-dough`, and the croissant
itself), and the two case halves the notes used to call empty both have things in them.

What is left is the **filled and finished** end of the list: nothing here is piped, glazed or assembled
from two components. There is no éclair, no fruit tart, no doughnut, and no turnover — every one of
which now needs only a filling and a finish, because the doughs under them are done.

## What it is missing

1. **Anpan**, **melon pan**, **cream pan**, **curry pan** — the Japanese bread case, all four. Shokupan
   is on the rack with nothing on the shelf beside it.

2. **Danish** (cheese, fruit) and **cinnamon twist** — the second column of the laminated case.

3. **Éclair** and **cream puff** — pastry-cream is written with nowhere to pipe it.

4. **Fruit tart** — pastry cream again, plus a sweet shell.

5. **Apple turnover** / **hand pie** — the case's most-reached-for item.

6. **Doughnut**, raised and glazed, and **cinnamon-sugar doughnut** — nothing fried in the whole
   collection.

7. **Black and white cookie** — twenty cookies are written and this one, the most named, is not.

8. **Rugelach**, **bialy**, **hamantaschen** — the Jewish bakery side. Babka, challah and rye are
    here; these three are not.

9. **Pão de queijo**, **coxinha**, **brigadeiro**, **quindim** — the Brazilian padaria, entirely
    absent, though caldo verde landed here.

10. **Katsu sando** — the bakery's own sandwich, and it needs shokupan, which is here.

11. **Mooncake** — seasonal, boxed, and the one item people buy at a bakery and never make.

12. **Chinese fruit cake** (fresh cream cake) — no whipped-cream layer cake exists, and this is what
    a Hong Kong counter sells for every birthday.

13. **Mochi donut** (pon de ring).

14. **Ma lai gao** — the steamed sponge that shares "sponge cake" with the baked one on the same board.

15. **Blueberry muffin** and **scone** — loaves are well covered; the single-serve morning bake is not.

16. **Sausage roll** / **ham and cheese croissant** — the savoury case.

17. **Sfogliatella** or **cannoli** — the Italian bakery's case (see also Pizzeria).

18. **A decorated cake, start to finish** — the case has twenty-one cakes and not one of them is
   frosted, filled and finished as one table. `whipped-cream` is written; a cream cheese frosting and
   a buttercream are not.

## Components it would need

- **Choux paste** — éclair, cream puff, profiterole, and the Brazilian *bomba*.
- **Cookie-lid topping** — the sugar-flour-fat crust that cracks into the grid. It is the *only*
  difference between a plain roll and a pineapple bun or a melon pan, and it is the thing nobody
  would think to look up.
- **Cream cheese frosting**, **American buttercream**, **royal icing** — six written items make one of
  these inline. One table each would serve all six and be findable on its own.
- **Streusel / crumb topping** — sour cream coffee cake makes it inline; four missing items need it.
- **Apricot glaze** and **sugar glaze** — how a case gets its shine.
- **Enriched fried-dough base** — doughnuts, loukoumades, zeppole, malasadas.
- **Tapioca-and-cheese base** — pão de queijo, and there is no wheatless bread here.
- **Catupiry-style cheese filling** — coxinha.
- **Filo handling and clarified butter** — baklava, and nothing filo exists.
- **Buttercream** — the one finish that turns eight of the written cakes into a case item. Nothing on
  the shelf frosts anything.
- **Pastry cream, piped** — `pastry-cream` is written and nothing is filled with it. The éclair, the
  fruit tart and the doughnut all wait on the same two operations.

## What it could not stock

- **A croissant as one table.** Lamination is a loop — fold, chill, turn, fold, chill, turn — not a
  chain of distinct operations, and the finished dough is then cut into a dozen crescents. Written
  honestly it is two recipes: the dough as its own table, and each shaped item as a short table that
  starts from it. Trying to hold both is exactly the split the build refuses.
- **Anything where one dough becomes a base and a lid.** A lattice tart, a two-crust pie, a stuffed
  turnover: one preparation feeding two later steps. The build says no on purpose. Write the dough
  once; write the pie as a recipe that consumes it.
- **A sourdough starter.** It never finishes. There is no final operation — it is a standing thing you
  keep and feed, and every branch must flow into one ending. sourdough-boule can assume it exists;
  the starter itself is prose.
- **A decorated whole cake.** Sponge, syrup, cream, fruit, crumb coat, finish — four tables and an
  assembly with no cooking in it. Sell the parts and pair them.
- **The two-case layout.** "Pan Dulce" on one side, "Pan Salado" on the other, sweet buns and savoury
  buns in different sections. That is the shop's floor plan, not a recipe.
- **Bread by the slice count.** "Cut into four, six or eight" and "three sizes of pão de queijo" are
  service facts. A table can hold the loaf; the knife is the customer's.
- **A tray of assorted cookies.** Twenty cookies on one table is twenty splits.
