# Bakery — what is missing

**91 recipes.** The biggest counter on the site by a factor of two, and the one where the shape of
the whole collection shows most clearly: forty-one of the ninety-one are cookies and bars. The case
is deep in things baked flat in a pan and empty of everything that needs a pastry shell, a lamination,
or a filling piped in.

danish, turnover) and **the savoury case** (sausage roll, curry pan, katsu sando, manakish).

## What it is missing

1. **Croissant** — nothing laminated exists anywhere in the collection. This is the loudest absence
   on the site's largest counter. With it: **pain au chocolat**, **almond croissant**.
2. **Egg custard tart** (dan tat, daan taat, Portuguese egg tart) — printed at Hong Kong, Japanese,
   Portuguese and Cantonese counters alike, and one of the Four Heavenly Kings. There are four
   custards in the case and not one shell to put one in.
3. **Pineapple bun** (bo lo bao) and **pineapple bun with butter** (bo lo yau) — the flagship of the
   Hong Kong bun case. Chiffon cake and castella are here; the buns beside them are not.
4. **Anpan**, **melon pan**, **cream pan**, **curry pan** — the Japanese bread case, all four. Shokupan
   is on the rack with nothing on the shelf beside it.
5. **Danish** (cheese, fruit) and **cinnamon twist** — the second column of the laminated case.
6. **Éclair** and **cream puff** — pastry-cream is written with nowhere to pipe it.
7. **Fruit tart** — pastry cream again, plus a sweet shell.
8. **Apple turnover** / **hand pie** — the case's most-reached-for item.
9. **Doughnut**, raised and glazed, and **cinnamon-sugar doughnut** — nothing fried in the whole
   collection.
10. **Black and white cookie** — twenty cookies are written and this one, the most named, is not.
11. **Rugelach**, **bialy**, **hamantaschen** — the Jewish bakery side. Babka, challah and rye are
    here; these three are not.
12. **Pão de queijo**, **coxinha**, **brigadeiro**, **quindim** — the Brazilian padaria, entirely
    absent, though caldo verde landed here.
13. **Manakish** — za'atar is written with nothing to spread it on but pita.
14. **Katsu sando** — the bakery's own sandwich, and it needs shokupan, which is here.
15. **Mooncake** — seasonal, boxed, and the one item people buy at a bakery and never make.
16. **Chinese fruit cake** (fresh cream cake) — no whipped-cream layer cake exists, and this is what
    a Hong Kong counter sells for every birthday.
17. **Mochi donut** (pon de ring).
18. **Ma lai gao** — the steamed sponge that shares "sponge cake" with the baked one on the same board.
19. **Blueberry muffin** and **scone** — loaves are well covered; the single-serve morning bake is not.
20. **Sausage roll** / **ham and cheese croissant** — the savoury case.
21. **Sfogliatella** or **cannoli** — the Italian bakery's case (see also Pizzeria).
22. **Baklava** — sold at the Arabic bakery beside the dip case (see also Shawarma Counter).

## Components it would need

- **Laminated dough** — a détrempe and a butter block, folded three times. Croissant, danish, pain au
  chocolat, campechana, oreja and puff-pastry turnovers all wait on it. Nothing in the collection is
  laminated.
- **Sweet shortcrust** (pâte sucrée) and **plain shortcrust** (pâte brisée) — the egg tart, the fruit
  tart, the turnover, the hand pie, the quiche. **No pastry shell of any kind exists on the site**,
  which is why four counters are missing their case.
- **Choux paste** — éclair, cream puff, profiterole, and the Brazilian *bomba*.
- **Red bean paste**, chunky (tsubu-an) and sieved (koshi-an) — anpan, sesame ball, mochi donut,
  mooncake.
- **Lotus seed paste** — mooncake.
- **Cookie-lid topping** — the sugar-flour-fat crust that cracks into the grid. It is the *only*
  difference between a plain roll and a pineapple bun or a melon pan, and it is the thing nobody
  would think to look up.
- **Sweetened whipped cream**, stabilised — Chinese fruit cake, layer cakes, the whole cream case.
- **Cream cheese frosting**, **American buttercream**, **royal icing** — six written items make one of
  these inline. One table each would serve all six and be findable on its own.
- **Frangipane** (almond cream) — almond croissant, fruit tart, bakewell.
- **Streusel / crumb topping** — sour cream coffee cake makes it inline; four missing items need it.
- **Apricot glaze** and **sugar glaze** — how a case gets its shine.
- **Enriched fried-dough base** — doughnuts, loukoumades, zeppole, malasadas.
- **Tapioca-and-cheese base** — pão de queijo, and there is no wheatless bread here.
- **Catupiry-style cheese filling** — coxinha.
- **Filo handling and clarified butter** — baklava, and nothing filo exists.

## What it could not stock

- **A croissant as one table.** Lamination is a loop — fold, chill, turn, fold, chill, turn — not a
  chain of distinct operations, and the finished dough is then cut into a dozen crescents. Written
  honestly it is two recipes: the dough as its own table, and each shaped item as a short table that
  starts from it. Trying to hold both is exactly the split the build refuses.
- **Anything where one dough becomes a base and a lid.** A lattice tart, a two-crust pie, a stuffed
  turnover: one preparation feeding two later steps. The build says no on purpose. Write the dough
  once; write the pie as a recipe that consumes it.
- **A sourdough starter.** It never finishes. There is no final operation — it is a standing thing you
  keep and feed, and every branch must flow into one ending. sourdough-boule can assume it exists;
  the starter itself is prose.
- **A decorated whole cake.** Sponge, syrup, cream, fruit, crumb coat, finish — four tables and an
  assembly with no cooking in it. Sell the parts and pair them.
- **The two-case layout.** "Pan Dulce" on one side, "Pan Salado" on the other, sweet buns and savoury
  buns in different sections. That is the shop's floor plan, not a recipe.
- **Bread by the slice count.** "Cut into four, six or eight" and "three sizes of pão de queijo" are
  service facts. A table can hold the loaf; the knife is the customer's.
- **A tray of assorted cookies.** Twenty cookies on one table is twenty splits.
