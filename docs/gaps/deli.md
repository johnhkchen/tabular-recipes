# Deli — what is missing

**62 recipes, 24 of them only here.** The slicer, the smoked-fish case, the salads-by-the-pound
list, the barrel and the spread case are all stocked — pastrami and corned beef off one cure,
belly lox, five salads, the sour dill, the sauerkraut, the schmaltz and the cream cheese.

What is missing is **ranks 12 to 25**, which are a coherent next ticket in three parts: the Italian
side (roast pork with broccoli rabe, hot pepper relish, Dutch crunch, swirl rye), the Polish side
(pierogi, kiełbasa, Leberkäse, kasha varnishkes) and the appetizing herrings. `egg-cream` is written
and is the site's third drink.

## What it is missing

1. **Italian combo / hoagie** — "#1 Italian Combo" on the board, and the reference records eleven regional
    names for the same sandwich. The best `aka` line available anywhere on this site.

2. **Philly cheesesteak** — wit or witout.

3. **Italian roast pork with broccoli rabe** — and **broccoli rabe** on its own, sold by the pound from the
    hot case.

4. **Hot pepper relish** (hoagie spread, long hots) — what keeps a cold sandwich from tasting flat.

5. **Dutch crunch roll** — the default San Francisco sandwich bread, and a bread this collection would be
    glad of: a rice-flour paste painted on before baking so the top cracks.

6. **Swirl rye** (marble rye) — `deli-rye-bread` is here; the marbled loaf in the case is a different item.

7. **Kasha varnishkes**, **kishka**, **noodle kugel**, **blintzes** — the hot case, four lines.

8. **Pierogi**, **uszka**, **żurek**, **bigos** — the Polish side. borscht is here alone.

9. **Kiełbasa biała** and **kiełbasa wiejska** — the fresh one you poach and the smoked horseshoe.

10. **Leberkäse** — baked in a pan, sliced thick, eaten warm. Writable, unlike the dry sausages.

11. **Pickled herring in cream** and **schmaltz herring**.

12. **Sloppy Joe (New Jersey)** — a *cold* triple-decker on thin rye, no ground beef, no relation to the
    school-lunch version. Worth writing for the disambiguation alone.

13. **Farmer cheese** — a component and a case item both.

## Components it would need

- **A curing brine** — salt, sugar, pink salt, aromatics, and a week. Pastrami, corned beef, dill pickles
  and sauerkraut all start from a brine, and **nothing brined or fermented exists on the site** except
  `turkey-brine` at the Smokehouse.
- **Pastrami rub and the steam finish** — cracked black pepper and coriander, then steamed until it gives.
  Those two operations are the entire difference between pastrami and corned beef, which is worth a table
  saying out loud.
- **Matzo balls** — and the schmaltz that makes them work.
- **Farmer cheese** — blintzes and rugelach fillings both want it, and the reference lists it as a
  by-the-pound item in its own right.
- **Knish dough** — stretched thin — and **potato filling** and **kasha filling**.
- **Hoagie roll** and **Dutch crunch topping paste** (rice flour, yeast, oil).
- **A lox cure** — salt and sugar, three days, no heat. A cure rather than a cook, and writable.
- **Sour dill brine** — a *lacto* ferment, not vinegar, and the difference between a sour and a
  quick-pickled cucumber is the whole reason the barrel exists.
- **Pierogi dough** and its fillings (potato-and-cheese, sauerkraut-and-mushroom).
- **Żur** — the fermented rye starter under white borscht. A standing preparation (see below).

## What it could not stock

- **Dry-cured anything.** Capicola, soppressata, mortadella, kabanosy, salceson. These are weeks in
  controlled humidity toward a target weight loss; the "operation" is hanging, and they do not converge to
  a final step so much as they stop. A table can hold a wet cure and a cook — pastrami and corned beef both
  work — but it cannot hold a curing chamber.
- **Cold-smoked fish.** Nova is cold-smoked below about 30°C for hours. The cure is writable and the smoke
  is equipment; a recipe that quietly substitutes hot smoke has made a different fish.
- **A sandwich.** This counter's central item is layers of things bought sliced. Pastrami on rye is two
  tables and one assembly — slice, pile, mustard, close — which is one operation and eight leaves: under
  the floor on operations and over it on the point. The honest form is components plus `pairs-with`.
- **Sold by the pound.** The case is priced by weight, and a tub of coleslaw scaled for a shop is not a
  cook's bowl.
- **Sliced to order.** "Hand-cut" and "paper-thin" are the item. The reference says pastrami is judged by
  whether it was cut by hand. A table can name the thickness; it cannot teach the knife.
- **The barrel.** A sour pickle barrel is a running ferment, topped up and never emptied. No final
  operation. A jar version is writable and is a different pickle — a good one, but not the barrel's.
- **A fermented rye starter (żur).** Same problem as a sourdough starter: it is kept, not finished.
- **The bread question before the filling question.** The reference notes the sandwich shop's identifying
  list is the *bread* list, and that about forty sandwiches were named with not one numbered. That is a
  menu grammar, and it lives in `aka` and in the shape of the counter page, not in a table.
