# Meat and Three — what is missing

**23 recipes.** The meat list is decently stocked with braises and the rice section is unusually good — but
**there is no cornbread**, and cornbread is what defines this counter: it comes whether you ask or not. The
vegetable list, which on a real board is nine or ten lines and is where three of your four choices come from,
has six entries and none of them is a green.

---

## What it has

**The meat list, rotating.** pot-roast · beef-stew · braised-short-ribs · beef-bourguignon · coq-au-vin ·
chicken-adobo

**The vegetable list** — remembering that on this board "vegetable" means anything that is not the meat.
cheese-grits · boston-baked-beans · hoppin-john · dirty-rice · jambalaya · jollof-rice

**Gravies.** turkey-pan-gravy · sausage-gravy · cheddar-cheese-sauce · cranberry-sauce

**Cornbread.** — nothing.

**Dessert.** bread-pudding · pecan-pie-bars · texas-sheet-cake · haupia

**The shelf.** cajun-seasoning · jerk-marinade · turkey-brine

**Shelved here from elsewhere.** beef-bourguignon and coq-au-vin are French bistro rather than cafeteria line;
chicken-adobo is Filipino (the reference records **Turo-Turo** as an archetype found and not shelved, and a
turo-turo steam table is genuinely this counter's cousin); jollof-rice is West African; haupia is Hawaiian.
Each is defensible as "one meat off a rotating list", and each is worth a maintainer's second look.

---

## What it is missing

1. **Cornbread** — "whether you ask or not". It is the counter's definition and it is not here. This is the
   single most conspicuous absence at this counter and one of the top five on the site.
2. **Collard greens** — the first line on every vegetable list, stewed soft with smoked pork, and the salty
   broth left in the pot is **pot likker**, which is what the cornbread is for. Turnip and mustard greens are
   near-interchangeable and belong in the same `aka`.
3. **Fried chicken** — the most-ordered line on the meat list, and there is nothing deep-fried anywhere on
   the site.
4. **Macaroni and cheese** — a vegetable on this board. `cheddar-cheese-sauce` is written with no macaroni.
5. **Candied yams** — not actual yams, baked down in butter and brown sugar until they glaze. Also the
   mashed-and-baked **sweet potato soufflé** version.
6. **Smothered pork chops** and **smothered chicken** — "smothered" is the method word of the entire room and
   no dish here carries it.
7. **Corn bread dressing** — an everyday side here, not a once-a-year thing, which is exactly the sort of fact
   worth recording.
8. **Baked turkey wings** and **oxtails** — two of the biggest plates on the board.
9. **Green beans cooked with pork**, **fried okra**, **stewed squash**, **black-eyed peas**, **butter beans**,
   **creamed corn** — the rest of the vegetable list, six lines.
10. **Meatloaf** and **country fried steak** — the other two meat-list regulars.
11. **Banana pudding**, **peach cobbler**, **sweet potato pie** — three desserts named in the reference for
    this counter and none of them written.
12. **Hot water cornbread** — cornmeal scalded with boiling water, patted flat, fried in a skillet. A
    different food from baked cornbread and a separate board line.
13. **Red beans and rice** — the Monday plate, a laundry-day habit that stuck to the printed menu.
14. **Gumbo** and **étouffée** — the Louisiana plate lunch's two anchors.
15. **Po-boy** — and the **hot roast beef / debris po-boy**, spooned on until the bread gives up.
16. **Boudin**, **cracklins**, **maque choux** — the meat-market counter's three.
17. **Chitterlings** — and hog maws in the same pot.
18. **Potato salad**, **coleslaw**, **deviled eggs** — the cold end of the line.
19. **Gumbo z'herbes** — built on stewed greens instead of seafood or chicken.
20. **Sweet tea** — the drink, again, and the site has none.

## Components it would need

- **Cornbread batter** — baked, and specifically the pour into a hot greased skillet that gives the fried edge.
  Also needed at the Smokehouse.
- **A smoked pork pot / ham hock stock** — the greens, the green beans, the black-eyed peas, the butter beans
  and the pot likker all come out of one pot. **One table, six sides**, and the highest-leverage missing
  component at this counter.
- **Dark roux** — flour and fat stirred forty-five minutes to the colour of an old penny. Gumbo and étouffée
  both need it, and it is the ideal case for a named timer, because it is forty-five minutes of your actual
  attention rather than a wait you can leave.
- **The trinity base** — onion, celery, bell pepper cooked down. `cajun-seasoning` covers the dry half; this
  is the wet half.
- **Buttermilk brine and seasoned flour dredge** — fried chicken, and country fried steak with it.
- **Cream gravy** — the plain white one. `sausage-gravy` is close and is not the same.
- **New Orleans French bread** — a shattering crust and a nearly hollow crumb, and it is a different loaf from
  every bread on the site. The po-boy is the bread.
- **Debris gravy** — the shreds that fell off into the pot, which is a second product of one roast (see below).
- **Pie shell** — sweet potato pie and pecan pie; see the Diner, where the same gap is recorded.
- **Cobbler topping** — a dropped biscuit or a batter; the reference says either.
- **Vanilla custard and wafers** — banana pudding.
- **Boudin filling** — pork, liver, cooked rice and seasoning, and the reference notes it is squeezed out of
  the skin rather than sliced, which is worth a line.
- **Candied yam glaze** — butter, brown sugar, warm spice, reduced.

## What it could not stock

- **Meat and three itself.** One meat off a rotating list, three off the vegetable list, cornbread included.
  That is four finished tables on a tray, and the *choosing* is the item. It is also the counter's name, which
  makes this the cleanest example on the site of a menu word that can never be a recipe.
- **The rotating list.** Thursday's meat. A schedule.
- **The steam table.** Forty portions cooked at nine and held until two, and the holding is part of what the
  food is. No home cook does it and no table should pretend to.
- **"Smothered", generically.** The word runs across chops, chicken, steak, cabbage, potatoes and greens
  alike — it names a method, not a dish. A table that tried to hold all six would be splitting the gravy.
  Write the onion gravy once and one smothered dish properly.
- **A pot of greens for the room.** Three bunches and a hock, two hours, and the **pot likker** is a second
  product sold alongside with cornbread for mopping. One preparation, two things, which the build refuses.
- **A roast that yields debris.** Same shape: the sliced beef is one menu item and the shreds in the gravy are
  another, off one pan.
- **Sweet tea by the urn.**
- **"Vegetable" as a category.** The reference's best line about this counter is that macaroni and cheese,
  candied yams, dressing and rice are all on the vegetable list, so it is easy to order three starches by
  accident. That is a fact about the board and it belongs on the counter page, not in a table.
