# Meat and Three — what is missing

**53 recipes, 27 of them only here.** The meat list rotates over six, the vegetable list runs to
twelve, cornbread is written twice, and the gravies and the dessert list are stocked. Five entries the
old note called missing — banana pudding, coleslaw, both cornbreads and the pie crust — were already on
the shelf when it was written.

What is missing is **the Louisiana column**: gumbo, red beans and rice, po-boy, boudin, chitterlings.
All five stand on a dark roux and a trinity base, neither of which is written, and the gap note's own
reading is that they are close to being a counter of their own.

## What it is missing

1. **Red beans and rice** — the Monday plate, a laundry-day habit that stuck to the printed menu.

2. **Gumbo** and **étouffée** — the Louisiana plate lunch's two anchors.

3. **Po-boy** — and the **hot roast beef / debris po-boy**, spooned on until the bread gives up.

4. **Boudin**, **cracklins**, **maque choux** — the meat-market counter's three.

5. **Chitterlings** — and hog maws in the same pot.

6. **Gumbo z'herbes** — built on stewed greens instead of seafood or chicken.

7. **Sweet tea** — the drink, again. `recipes/drinks/` holds three and none of them is brewed.

---

## Components it would need

- **Dark roux** — flour and fat stirred forty-five minutes to the colour of an old penny. Gumbo and étouffée
  both need it, and it is the ideal case for a named timer, because it is forty-five minutes of your actual
  attention rather than a wait you can leave.
- **The trinity base** — onion, celery, bell pepper cooked down. `cajun-seasoning` covers the dry half; this
  is the wet half.
- **Buttermilk brine and seasoned flour dredge** — fried chicken, and country fried steak with it.
- **New Orleans French bread** — a shattering crust and a nearly hollow crumb, and it is a different loaf from
  every bread on the site. The po-boy is the bread.
- **Debris gravy** — the shreds that fell off into the pot, which is a second product of one roast (see below).
- **Boudin filling** — pork, liver, cooked rice and seasoning, and the reference notes it is squeezed out of
  the skin rather than sliced, which is worth a line.

## What it could not stock

- **Meat and three itself.** One meat off a rotating list, three off the vegetable list, cornbread included.
  That is four finished tables on a tray, and the *choosing* is the item. It is also the counter's name, which
  makes this the cleanest example on the site of a menu word that can never be a recipe.
- **The rotating list.** Thursday's meat. A schedule.
- **The steam table.** Forty portions cooked at nine and held until two, and the holding is part of what the
  food is. No home cook does it and no table should pretend to.
- **"Smothered", generically.** The word runs across chops, chicken, steak, cabbage, potatoes and greens
  alike — it names a method, not a dish. A table that tried to hold all six would be splitting the gravy.
  Write the onion gravy once and one smothered dish properly.
- **A pot of greens for the room.** Three bunches and a hock, two hours, and the **pot likker** is a second
  product sold alongside with cornbread for mopping. One preparation, two things, which the build refuses.
- **A roast that yields debris.** Same shape: the sliced beef is one menu item and the shreds in the gravy are
  another, off one pan.
- **Sweet tea by the urn.**
- **"Vegetable" as a category.** The reference's best line about this counter is that macaroni and cheese,
  candied yams, dressing and rice are all on the vegetable list, so it is easy to order three starches by
  accident. That is a fact about the board and it belongs on the counter page, not in a table.
